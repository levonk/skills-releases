# Vehicle Smog and CARB Compliance

## Failure Mode

A vehicle purchased at auction in another state may not be registerable
in California or other strict-emissions states. The vehicle may lack
CARB (California Air Resources Board) certification, may not pass a
smog test, or may have an out-of-state title that requires a VIN
verification and smog check before registration. The buyer discovers
this only after attempting to register the vehicle — by which point
the auction sale is final.

## Practice

**Before recommending an auction vehicle to a user in a
strict-emissions state, check smog compliance.**

1. **Check the emissions label**: The label under the hood indicates
   whether the vehicle is "50-state" (CARB compliant) or "49-state"
   (EPA only). 49-state vehicles cannot be registered in California.

2. **Budget for smog repairs**: Auction vehicles are sold as-is and
   may not pass smog. Budget $200–$1,500+ for smog-related repairs
   (catalytic converter, O2 sensors, EGR valve).

3. **Check diesel regulations**: California has strict diesel emissions
   rules (DPF, DEF). Pre-2010 diesels may be non-compliant.

4. **Check other strict states**: New York, Illinois (certain
   counties), Colorado, Nevada, Oregon (certain areas) also have
   emissions testing requirements.

5. **Factor in DMV fees**: Out-of-state titles require a VIN
   verification and smog check before California registration. Add
   DMV fees and inspection time to the total acquisition cost.

## Why

California's emissions standards are stricter than federal EPA
standards. A vehicle that is legal in 49 states may be illegal to
register in California. Auction vehicles are sold as-is with no
emissions guarantee — unlike a dealership, which must ensure the
vehicle passes smog before sale in California.

The financial impact can be severe: a vehicle that cannot pass smog
cannot be registered, cannot be legally driven, and is effectively
worthless to a California buyer (unless sold for parts or exported).
