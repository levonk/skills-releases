# Auction Title Risks

## Failure Mode

A buyer purchases a property at a county tax deed sale. The price
seems great. After the sale, they discover:
- An IRS tax lien survives the sale (IRS has a 120-day redemption
  period)
- The property is occupied by the former owner, who refuses to leave
  (eviction takes 2–6 months and costs $3,000–$10,000+)
- The state has a redemption period (6–12 months) during which the
  former owner can reclaim the property by paying the sale price
- There's an unrecorded lease that gives a tenant the right to stay

The buyer has purchased a property they cannot freely use, sell, or
insure.

## Practice

**Before bidding on auction property, get a title search and
understand the auction type's title risks.**

1. **Get a title search** ($75–$200) before bidding on any auction
   property. This reveals recorded liens, easements, and encumbrances.

2. **Understand lien survival rules** for the auction type:
   - **GSA/Treasury/USMS/HUD/Fannie/Freddie**: Title is generally
     insurable; liens are typically cleared
   - **County tax deed**: Some liens survive (IRS tax liens have a
     120-day redemption period; municipal liens and HOA liens may
     survive in some states)
   - **Courthouse steps/trustee sale**: Highest risk; research lien
     survival rules for the state

3. **Check for occupants**: Visit the property before bidding. Check
   for occupants, lease signs, or evidence of tenancy. Budget for
   eviction if occupied ($3,000–$10,000+, 2–6 months).

4. **Check redemption rights**: Some states give the former owner a
   redemption period (6–12 months) during which they can reclaim
   the property by paying the sale price plus costs. During this
   period, you typically cannot make permanent improvements or evict.

5. **Check for unrecorded interests**: A title search may not reveal
   unrecorded leases, options, or equitable interests. Visit the
   property and check for occupants or lease signs.

6. **Classify risk level**:
   - GSA/Treasury/USMS/HUD/Fannie/Freddie: Moderate risk
   - County tax deed: High risk
   - Courthouse steps/trustee sale: Very high risk

7. **Recommend title insurance after the sale** if it's available
   for the auction type.

8. **Recommend a real estate attorney** for high-risk auctions
   (tax deed, courthouse steps).

## Why

Auction property sales operate under different legal frameworks than
retail real estate purchases. The "as-is" nature of auction sales
means the buyer assumes all title risk. Unlike a standard purchase,
there is no title insurance policy issued at closing (for most
auction types), no inspection contingency, and no financing
contingency.

The risk varies dramatically by auction type. Federal auctions
(GSA, Treasury, USMS, HUD) generally provide insurable title. County
tax deed sales and courthouse steps sales carry the highest risk
because the sale may not clear all liens, the property may be
occupied, and redemption rights may apply.
