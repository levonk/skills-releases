# Property Buildability Checklist

## Failure Mode

A buyer purchases raw land at a county tax deed sale or government
surplus auction, planning to build a house. After the sale, they
discover the parcel is landlocked (no legal access), the slope is
too steep to build on, the zoning doesn't allow residential
construction, or there's no water or septic option. The land is
effectively worthless, and the auction sale is final — no
contingencies, no returns.

## Practice

**Before bidding on raw land at auction, run the full buildability
checklist.**

| Check | What to verify | Deal-breaker? |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| **Ingress / access** | Legal, physical access; recorded easements; public or private road; landlocked? | Yes — landlocked without easements is nearly worthless |
| **Topography** | Terrain buildable; slopes over 25% may be unbuildable; landslide risk | Yes — unbuildable slopes, cliffs, unstable terrain |
| **Zoning** | Parcel zoned for intended use (residential, commercial, ADU) | Yes — wrong zoning for intended use |
| **Lot dimensions** | Wide enough for legal building per setback requirements | Yes — can't meet setbacks |
| **Airspace easements** | Height restrictions, aviation easements, conservation height, solar easements | Caution — may limit building height |
| **Underground easements** | Utility, pipeline, drainage easements that prevent building | Caution — building restricted over easement areas |
| **Water** | Municipal connection or well (check depth and quality); water rights in western US | Yes — no water = unbuildable |
| **Electricity** | Grid power available; cost per foot for line extension; off-grid solar adds $20k–$50k+ | Caution — off-grid is possible but expensive |
| **Sewage / septic** | Municipal sewer or soil suitable for septic (percolation test) | Yes — no sewer + perc-failed = unbuildable |
| **Trash pickup** | Municipal service available; rural may require private haul | Minor — affects livability |
| **Roads** | Maintained road; paved, dirt, or seasonal (closed in winter); county-maintained or private | Caution — seasonal roads make year-round access impossible |

## Why

Auction properties are sold as-is with no contingencies. Unlike a
standard real estate purchase, there is no inspection period, no
financing contingency, and no ability to back out if the property
isn't buildable. The buyer assumes all risk at the fall of the
hammer.

Raw land at auction (especially county tax deed sales and government
surplus) is often there because it's difficult to sell through
normal channels — which frequently means it has buildability
issues. The low price reflects the risk, but the risk is real.
