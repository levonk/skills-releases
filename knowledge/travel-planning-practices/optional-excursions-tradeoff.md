# Optional Excursions Trade-Off

Optional excursions are activities sold *on top of* the base
itinerary, usually mid-trip by the guide. This section causes the
most regret in all of travel documentation — the detail that the
headline experience costs extra and needs advance booking often lives
here, under a heading like "Optional Inclusions," late in the
document.

## The Two Structural Problems

1. **Priced for convenience, not value.** Operator optionals carry a
   markup over equivalent independent offerings — a $48 cave tour can
   have a near-identical $20 counterpart at the same dock. Always
   price-check high-ticket optionals independently *before* the trip,
   not at the dock.
2. **"Free time" can be a sales funnel.** A "morning at leisure" may
   exist precisely so the guide can sell an optional excursion to
   fill it. Read free-time blocks next to the optionals list — the
   pairing is often deliberate.

## The Decision Rule

- **Book the operator's optional** when it requires special access
  (a private after-hours museum visit) or tricky logistics (a
  permit-controlled site), or when you simply don't want the hassle —
  the markup buys peace of mind.
- **Do it yourself** when it's a standard, easily-arranged activity
  in a touristed area — a sunset cruise, a cooking class, a beach
  day. Lower price, smaller group.
- **Note advance-booking requirements.** Some optionals must be
  booked days ahead (e.g., 72 hours). Missing the window converts a
  "choice" into a closed door — find these deadlines during the
  [Pre-Booking Verification Workflow](pre-booking-verification-workflow.md).
