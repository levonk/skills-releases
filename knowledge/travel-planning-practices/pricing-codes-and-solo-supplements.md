# Pricing Codes and Solo Supplements

The header block carries the boring fields that matter most: booking
reference, travel dates with day count, and pax count. They define
what the quoted price actually buys.

## The Codes

- **Booking reference / file number** — quote it in every email.
  Agencies process hundreds of files; a name gets you nowhere, a
  reference number gets you answers.
- **"Pax"** means passengers. Pricing is almost always *per person,
  based on double occupancy* — the headline number assumes two people
  sharing a room.
- **Single supplement** — the solo-traveler surcharge, commonly
  30–60% on top of the per-person price on escorted tours, sometimes
  100%. Some operators waive it on select departures to fill rooms.
  Always ask: *"Do you have any single-supplement-waived
  departures?"* before accepting the quoted solo price.
- **Currency** — check which currency the quote is in. A safari
  quoted in USD vs ZAR can swing ~10% with the exchange rate by the
  time the final balance is due months after the deposit.

## Verifying What's Actually Booked

- **Ask for e-ticket numbers when flights are included.** A
  13-character ticket number means the flight is actually issued, not
  just "held." Held flights can evaporate or be repriced upward; issued
  tickets exist.
- **Check whether the day count includes travel days** — see
  [Pace and Day-Count Traps](pace-and-day-count-traps.md). A "7-day
  tour" where two days are mostly planes sells 5 days of touring.
