# Itinerary Verb Decoding

The day-by-day section is written in a coded dialect where each verb
carries a precise operational meaning. Decoding it prevents the most
common disappointment: expecting to go *inside* a landmark the
itinerary only promised to drive past.

## The Verb Table

| Phrase | What it actually means |
|--------|------------------------|
| "Visit" | You go inside, usually with admission included |
| "See" / "view" | You look at it — often from the bus or a viewpoint. No entry |
| "Photo stop" | 10–15 minutes, engine running |
| "Drive past" / "panoramic tour" | You do not get out. A "panoramic city tour of Florence" can mean circling it on a coach |
| "At leisure" / "free time" | Nothing is planned and nothing is paid — fine if wanted, a problem if a guide was expected |
| "Orientation walk" | A short, free, get-your-bearings stroll — not a real guided tour |

## Practice

- **Count the verbs that cost money.** A day built on "see," "photo
  stop," and "panoramic" is a transit-and-look day, not a touring
  day — value it accordingly when comparing against
  [Optional Excursions](optional-excursions-tradeoff.md) and other
  operators.
- **Flag every "at leisure" block.** Each one is simultaneously a gap
  to fill and a possible
  [sales funnel](optional-excursions-tradeoff.md) for guide-sold
  optionals. Decide in advance what fills it.
- **Translate the day before judging the trip.** Rewrite each day in
  plain terms ("morning: drive by cathedral, 15-min photo stop;
  afternoon: free") before comparing itineraries — this is step one
  of the
  [Pre-Booking Verification Workflow](pre-booking-verification-workflow.md).
