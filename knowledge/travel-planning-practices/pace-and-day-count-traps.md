# Pace and Day-Count Traps

Two easy numbers are routinely misread: the day count in the title,
and the hotel check-in count hidden in the day-by-day.

## The Day-Count Trap

A "10-day tour" with an overnight long-haul flight on Day 1 and an
early departure on Day 10 is effectively 8 days. **Count the touring
days, not the calendar days** — the product being sold is touring
days, and the title inflates them.

## The Pace Signal

- **Count the hotel check-ins.** Five hotels in seven nights means
  packing, unpacking, and lobby-waiting nearly every day. That's a
  fine trade for coverage and a brutal one for relaxation — decide
  which trip you wanted before booking, not after.
- **Combine with transit math.** A fast hotel cadence plus
  [long drive days](transit-time-verification.md) compounds: every
  transit day also carries a checkout, a luggage gap, and a check-in
  (see [Check-In/Out Timing Gaps](check-in-out-timing-gaps.md)).
- **Pace is a preference, not a defect.** The question isn't whether
  fast pace is bad — it's whether the pace the document describes
  matches the trip being sold to you. Fast-cover itineraries are
  legitimately great for survey trips; they are a poor fit for
  travelers expecting downtime.
