# Travel Planning Practices

A compounding knowledge base for the traveler side of a booked trip —
reading, decoding, and verifying the documents an operator or agent hands
you before money changes hands. The organizing insight is that a formal
travel itinerary is a sales document on page one and a contract in the
fine print: every section has a coded meaning, and the cost of misreading
it is paid at the dock, not at the desk.

Each concept captures a specific reading discipline — how to identify the
itinerary type, decode the day-by-day verbs and meal codes, find the
excluded costs, price the conditional and optional items, and turn the
whole document into a set of written questions answered before the
deposit becomes non-refundable.

The practices are synthesized from consumer travel-literacy sources and
are general — applicable to escorted tours, private custom itineraries,
self-guided packages, and cruise shore-excursion sheets — not specific
to any operator or destination.

## Concepts

### Reading the Document

* [Overview](overview.md) — Synthesis of how the itinerary-reading disciplines fit together across the booking lifecycle
* [Itinerary Types](itinerary-types.md) — Escorted group, private custom/DMC, self-guided FIT, and cruise excursion sheets set different baselines for what "included" means; identify the type first
* [Itinerary Verb Decoding](itinerary-verb-decoding.md) — "Visit" vs "see/view" vs "photo stop" vs "drive past/panoramic" vs "at leisure" vs "orientation walk" — the verbs tell you what actually happens
* [Meal Codes and Gap Budgeting](meal-codes-and-gap-budgeting.md) — B/L/D letters per day; missing letters are unbudgeted meals, priced by destination
* [Check-In/Out Timing Gaps](check-in-out-timing-gaps.md) — Hotel check-in is typically 2–3 PM and checkout ~noon; arrival/departure timing creates luggage gaps the itinerary glosses over

### Cost and Pricing Traps

* [Pricing Codes and Solo Supplements](pricing-codes-and-solo-supplements.md) — "Pax" and per-person double occupancy, the 30–60% single supplement and waived departures, quote currency, and e-ticket verification
* [Inclusions and Exclusions Analysis](inclusions-exclusions-analysis.md) — Read the exclusions first: visas, gratuities, city taxes, insurance, drinks, and self-booked-flight transfers are routinely outside the headline price
* [Optional Excursions Trade-Off](optional-excursions-tradeoff.md) — Optionals are priced for convenience and "free time" can be a sales funnel; book the operator's only for access or logistics
* [Transit Time Verification](transit-time-verification.md) — Itineraries compress geography; verify the two longest drive days on a map — more than ~5 hours of transit makes it a transit day
* [Pace and Day-Count Traps](pace-and-day-count-traps.md) — Travel days inflate the day count and nightly hotel changes define the pace; count touring days and check-ins, not brochure days
* [Hotel Substitution and Guide Model](hotel-substitution-and-guide-model.md) — "4-star or similar" permits substitution and per-city guides are not an escort; get hotel names and identify the guiding model

### Risk and Recourse

* [Conditional Activities and Force Majeure](conditional-activities-and-force-majeure.md) — "Weather permitting" is a disclaimer; find the recourse clause — "no refund for weather" is generally enforceable
* [Live Itinerary Version Control](live-itinerary-version-control.md) — App and WhatsApp itineraries can change silently after payment; screenshot the version you booked and get terms in a savable format

### Verification Workflow

* [Pre-Booking Verification Workflow](pre-booking-verification-workflow.md) — Rebuild the itinerary as a two-column "what it says / what it means" table, then send numbered questions and get written answers before the deposit deadline
* [Well-Written Itinerary Benchmark](well-written-itinerary-benchmark.md) — The positive checklist: named hotels, meal codes, realistic drive times, priced optionals, conditional recourse, cancellation schedule, guiding model, and reachable contacts
