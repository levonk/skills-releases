# Conditional Activities and Force Majeure

The most-anticipated experiences on earth are the ones an operator
can't promise: the Northern Lights, a clear summit view, a
monsoon-dependent rafting run, peak-bloom flowers. The conditional
language around these tells you exactly who eats the risk.

## Disclaimer vs Recourse

- **"Weather permitting" / "subject to conditions"** is a
  *disclaimer* — it warns the activity may not happen. It says
  nothing about what happens next.
- **The *recourse* clause** is what you want: Does the operator offer
  a re-attempt the next night? A substitute activity? A partial
  credit? A traveler-friendly Iceland itinerary says "if the aurora
  is not visible, a second attempt is offered on a subsequent night,
  subject to availability." A weak one says only "viewing is
  weather-dependent and not guaranteed" — a polite way of saying you
  may pay full price and see nothing.

## What "No Refund for Weather" Means Legally

When an operator can't deliver because of weather, that usually
falls under a **force majeure** clause — events outside their
control. In most jurisdictions and contracts, force majeure releases
the operator from refunding a missed weather-dependent activity,
*provided they still ran everything within their control.* They
didn't cancel the trip; nature did. You paid for the attempt and the
logistics, not the guaranteed sighting.

## Practical Rules

- **Don't build a trip around a single non-guaranteed highlight**
  unless you've budgeted the time and money to try more than once.
- **Travel insurance rarely covers disappointment.** It covers
  cancellation for covered reasons, not "we didn't see the aurora."
- **Ask before booking:** "If [the conditional activity] doesn't run,
  what specifically happens?" Get the answer in writing — a vague
  answer is its own red flag (see
  [Pre-Booking Verification Workflow](pre-booking-verification-workflow.md)).
- **"Subject to" language is not boilerplate.** "Subject to
  availability," "order of activities may change" — flag every
  conditional highlight and know which parts of the trip can move or
  vanish.
