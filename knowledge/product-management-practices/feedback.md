# Giving and Receiving Feedback

Feedback is one of the PM's most-used tools. The curated sources below cover
three distinct practices: the discipline of giving positive feedback
generously, the concept of "thirty percent feedback" for early-stage work, and
the mechanics of running a design critique that produces improvement rather
than defensiveness.

## Positive Feedback

Zach Holman's "You won't regret positive feedback" argues that most people
under-deliver positive feedback and over-deliver criticism. The advice is
simple: when you notice something good, say so — you will not regret
it.[^holman-positive-feedback]

## Thirty Percent Feedback

Jason Freedman's "thirty percent feedback" is a framework for giving feedback
on early-stage work. The idea: when something is 30% done, the feedback should
focus on direction and big-picture questions, not details. Matching the type
of feedback to the stage of the work prevents premature
critique.[^freedman-thirty-percent-feedback]

## Design Critiques

Scott Berkun's "How to run a design critique" is the canonical guide to
structuring a critique session — setting expectations, framing the work,
and guiding the conversation toward actionable improvement rather than
opinion.[^berkun-design-critique]
