# Product Leadership

Product leadership is the practice of leading through influence rather than
authority. The PM rarely has direct reports, yet is responsible for aligning
engineering, design, marketing, sales, and executive stakeholders around a
shared direction. The curated sources below cover the mechanics of influence,
stakeholder management, and the qualities that distinguish the best PMs from
the rest.

The recurring theme is that leadership in product management is earned
through clarity of thought, quality of communication, and consistency of
judgment — not granted by title.

## Influence and Stakeholder Management

Sachin Rekhi's "influence without authority" is the canonical guide to
leading when you have no formal power.[^rekhi-influence-without-authority]
Marty Cagan's "stakeholder management" frames it as a core PM
competency.[^cagan-stakeholder-management] Bruce McCarthy's "court scribe or
Hand of the King" uses a Game of Thrones metaphor to ask whether the PM is a
note-taker or a true leader.[^mccarthy-court-scribe-or-hand-of-king] Jackie
Bavaro's "how to be persuasive" offers practical tactics.[^bavaro-how-to-be-persuasive]
Walter Chen's "Bad Managers Talk, Good Managers Write" argues that written
communication is the lever for scaling influence.[^chen-bad-managers-talk-good-managers-write]
Steven Sinofsky's "management cliches that work" identifies the sayings that
actually hold up in practice.[^sinofsky-management-cliches-that-work]

## The Top 1%

Ian McAllister's Quora answer on what distinguishes the top 1% of PMs from
the top 10% is the most-cited single piece on PM
excellence.[^mcallister-top-1-percent-pm] Edward Ho's answer on what makes a
great PM at Google adds a company-specific lens.[^ho-great-pm-at-google]
Chris Wetherell's answer on innate traits of product leaders rounds out the
Quora trilogy.[^wetherell-innate-traits-product-leaders] Ben Horowitz's
"Good Product Manager, Bad Product Manager" is the foundational
text.[^horowitz-good-pm-bad-pm]

## Leadership Lessons and Hiring

Adam Nash contributes two essays: "be a great product leader" and "top 10
product leadership lessons".[^nash-be-a-great-product-leader][^nash-top-10-product-leadership-lessons]
Todd Jackson's "top hacks from a PM behind two of tech's hottest products"
offers field-tested tactics.[^jackson-top-hacks-pm] Mike Sukmanowsky's
"Musings on Product Management" and Erik Torenberg's "What makes a great
Product Manager?" add further reflections.[^sukmanowsky-musings-on-pm][^torenberg-what-makes-great-pm]
Sutha Kamal's "Hiring a great product leader" approaches the topic from the
hiring side.[^kamal-hiring-great-product-leader] Mike Su's "How a football
coach taught me to product manage like a boss" draws an unexpected
analogy.[^su-football-coach-product-manage]
