# Roadmaps, Planning, and Prioritization

Roadmaps are one of the most debated artifacts in product management. The
curated sources below capture the full spectrum: from those who argue you
should abandon feature-based roadmaps entirely to those who defend the roadmap
as a communication tool when done right. The common thread is that a roadmap
is not a list of features with dates — it is a strategic document that
communicates vision, priorities, and the reasoning behind them.

Prioritization is the harder problem behind every roadmap. The sources below
cover frameworks for saying no, the opportunity backlog as an alternative to
the feature backlog, and the anti-patterns that make roadmaps fail.

## Roadmap Anti-Patterns

Marty Cagan's "seven deadly sins of product planning" catalogs the most common
failure modes.[^cagan-seven-deadly-sins] Bruce McCarthy's "dirty dozen
roadmap roadblocks" extends the list with organizational
obstacles.[^mccarthy-dirty-dozen-roadblocks] Rich Mironov warns against
"magical thinking and the zero-sum roadmap" — the delusion that everything can
be done simultaneously.[^mironov-magical-thinking] Joel Spolsky's "Software
Inventory" reframes unbuilt features as inventory — a cost, not an
asset.[^spolsky-software-inventory]

## Saying No and Strategic Prioritization

Des Traynor's "product strategy means saying no" is the canonical statement
that strategy is defined by what you choose not to do.[^traynor-strategy-means-saying-no]
Bruce McCarthy argues priorities must start with strategic
goals.[^mccarthy-priorities-strategic-goals] Marty Cagan's "inconvenient truth
about product" and "opportunity backlog" reframe the backlog as a place to
prioritize opportunities, not features.[^cagan-inconvenient-truth][^cagan-opportunity-backlog]
Ian McAllister's Quora answer surveys the best prioritization
frameworks.[^mcallister-prioritize-features]

## Vision Over Features

Bruce McCarthy argues roadmaps should focus on vision and benefits, not
features.[^mccarthy-roadmaps-vision-benefits] Teresa Torres and Andrea Saez
both argue for dropping feature-based roadmaps in favor of outcome-oriented
alternatives.[^torres-drop-feature-based-roadmaps][^saez-how-to-build-roadmap]
Des Traynor's "before you plan your product roadmap" is a pre-roadmap
checklist.[^traynor-before-you-plan-roadmap] Colin Rand takes the contrarian
view: don't publish a roadmap at all.[^rand-dont-publish-roadmap] David
Heinemeier Hansson agrees — you don't need one.[^hansson-you-dont-need-roadmap]

## Focus and Frameworks

Kenton Kivetsu's pyramid model maps PM focus across levels of the
product.[^kivetsu-pyramid-model] Sachin Rekhi's daily-focus question — "how am
I going to move my product forward today?" — is a micro-level prioritization
practice.[^rekhi-move-product-forward] Adam Nash's "three feature buckets" is
a planning framework that categorizes features by type.[^nash-three-feature-buckets]
Kenneth Norton's "Babe Ruth and feature lists" uses a baseball analogy to
explain why feature lists fail.[^norton-babe-ruth-feature-lists] Kris Gale
identifies the one cost engineers and PMs don't consider: the cost of
context-switching and fragmentation.[^gale-one-cost-engineers-pms] Mark
Stephan's "Making a Product Map" and Adam Juda's "Visual Guide to Feature
Selection" offer visual approaches to roadmap
communication.[^stephan-making-a-product-map][^juda-visual-guide-feature-selection]
