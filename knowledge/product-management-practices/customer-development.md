# Customer Development

Customer development is the practice of getting out of the building and
testing assumptions about who your customers are and what they need. It
complements product management: where PM focuses on what to build, customer
development focuses on validating whether the problem is real and the solution
is desired.

The curated sources below cover the mechanics of customer interviews, the
relationship between customer development and product management, and the
critical mindset shift that an MVP is a discovery tool, not a product.

## Listening and Interviewing

Kenneth Norton's "How to Listen to Customers" is the canonical guide to
absorbing what customers actually say versus what you expect to
hear.[^norton-how-to-listen] Jason Evanish provides a practical structure for
customer development interviews — the questions to ask and the ones to
avoid.[^evanish-cd-interviews] Wade Foster adds the necessary counterweight:
customers are not always right, and part of the job is interpreting, not just
transcribing.[^foster-customer-always-right]

## MVP as Discovery

Kevin Dewalt reframes the MVP: it is about discovery, not product. The goal is
learning, not shipping a minimal version of the final thing.[^dewalt-mvp-discovery]
Cindy Alvarez connects customer development to the PM role, showing how the two
disciplines interlock.[^alvarez-cd-and-pm]
