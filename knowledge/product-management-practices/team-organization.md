# Team Organization

How product management is organized within a company shapes what the PM can
actually do. The curated sources below cover the organizational models
(matrix vs. dedicated teams), the characteristics of good and bad product
teams, and the personal time-management challenge that every PM faces.

The structural question — whether PMs report into engineering, marketing, or
a dedicated product organization — determines how much authority and
influence the role carries. The personal question — how a PM manages their
own time across the maker's and manager's schedules — determines whether they
can do the work at all.

## Organizational Models

Mark Tattersall's "Enter the Matrix" examines the matrix model for organizing
product management and its tradeoffs.[^tattersall-enter-the-matrix] Marty
Cagan's three essays cover the full landscape: product organizational
structure, the characteristics that separate good product teams from bad
ones, and his recommendation for the best PM
model.[^cagan-product-organizational-structure][^cagan-good-product-team-bad-product-team][^cagan-best-pm-model]

## Time Management

Paul Graham's "Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule" is the foundational essay
on why PMs — who live on the manager's schedule of meetings — must protect
maker time for deep work.[^graham-makers-schedule] Jackie Bavaro's "Take back
your day" applies this specifically to PMs, offering tactics for managing
time across the competing demands of the role.[^bavaro-take-back-your-day]
