# Building Great Products

Building great products is less a formula than a set of reinforcing
principles: simplicity, quality, focus on the customer, and the discipline to
ship a coherent slice rather than a sprawling feature set. The curated sources
below distill these principles from practitioners who have shipped products at
scale.

The throughline is that quality and simplicity are not tradeoffs but
preconditions for adoption. A product that is simple but not useful fails; a
product that is useful but not simple also fails. The job of the PM is to find
the slice that is both.

## Simplicity and the Perfect Slice

Adam Nash channels Einstein: make things as simple as possible, but not
simpler.[^nash-as-simple-as-possible] Alastair Lee's "power of the perfect
slice" argues that shipping a complete, coherent end-to-end experience beats
shipping a broader but incomplete one.[^lee-perfect-slice] Slava Akhmechet's
"how to build great products" is a foundational essay on the mindset behind
product excellence.[^akhmechet-how-to-build-great-products]

## Quality and Customer Love

Julie Zhuo argues that quality is not a tradeoff you make against speed — it
is the baseline.[^zhuo-quality-not-tradeoff] Laurence McCahill's ten essential
steps and Avid Larizadeh's ten principles both offer checklists for building
products customers genuinely love.[^mccahill-10-essential-steps][^larizadeh-ten-principles]
Kevin Hale's startup lecture on building products users love rounds out the
set with a practitioner's field guide.[^hale-how-to-build-products-users-love]
