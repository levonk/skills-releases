# Shipping and Measuring Products

Shipping is the moment of truth for a product team. Everything upstream —
discovery, requirements, design, engineering — converges on the decision to
release. The curated sources below cover the philosophy of shipping, the
metrics that matter, and the frameworks for measuring user experience after
launch.

The throughline is that shipping is itself a feature: the ability to deliver
reliably and on time is a competitive advantage. But shipping without
measurement is just activity. The PM must define success before building,
measure it after launching, and use the data to inform the next cycle.

## The Philosophy of Shipping

Steven Sinofsky's "Shipping is a feature" is the canonical statement that the
ability to ship is itself a product capability.[^sinofsky-shipping-is-a-feature]
Ben Kamens's "Shipping beats perfection explained" elaborates on why the
tradeoff favors shipping.[^kamens-shipping-beats-perfection] Mark Suster's
"how to deliver more software projects on time" is a practical guide to
estimation and delivery.[^suster-deliver-on-time] Adam Nash's "great product
leaders win games" uses a sports analogy to frame shipping as a team
discipline.[^nash-great-product-leaders-win] Wade Foster's "lost art of the
soft launch" argues for the value of gradual, low-risk
releases.[^foster-lost-art-soft-launch]

## Defining and Measuring Success

Jackie Bavaro's "always be defining success" is the discipline of stating what
success looks like before you build.[^bavaro-always-be-defining-success] Josh
Elman's "the only metric that matters" argues for ruthless focus on a single
north-star metric.[^elman-only-metric-that-matters] Paul Adams's "the blind
Product Manager" is a cautionary tale about PMs who cannot see their own
data.[^adams-blind-pm] Rick Klau's "measure twice, cut once" applies the
carpenter's wisdom to product measurement.[^klau-measure-twice-cut-once]
Drew Dillon's answer on mobile product metrics surveys the field for
mobile-specific measurement.[^dillon-mobile-product-metrics]

## The HEART Framework

Kenny Lee's overview of Google's HEART framework (Happiness, Engagement,
Adoption, Retention, Task success) provides a structured approach to
measuring UX quality across these five dimensions.[^lee-google-heart-framework]
