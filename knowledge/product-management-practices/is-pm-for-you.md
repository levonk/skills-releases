# Is PM For You?

Before pursuing product management, understand what the role demands and
whether your disposition fits. The curated sources address three questions:
what makes a PM, what are the downsides, and do you need to be technical.

## The DNA of Product Management

Hunter Walk frames the PM's DNA as a combination of product sense, leadership,
and execution.[^walk-dna-of-pm] Kenneth Norton's "How to hire a Product
Manager?" is the canonical hiring brief — it describes the attributes that
separate people who can do the job from people who want the
title.[^norton-how-to-hire-pm]

## The Dark Side

Ellen Chisa's "The Dark Side of PM" is the honest counterweight — the role
involves ambiguity, blame without authority, and emotional
labor.[^chisa-dark-side] Read this before committing to the career path.

## The Technical Question

Two perspectives frame the debate:

- Ellen Chisa asks "Do I need to be technical to be a PM?" — the answer is
  nuanced: you need technical literacy, not necessarily coding
  skill.[^chisa-need-technical]
- Hunter Walk's "Ode to a Non-Technical Product Manager" celebrates the
  non-technical PM perspective as a strength, not a
  deficit.[^walk-ode-non-technical]

Kenton Kivetsu distills the discussion into three skillsets for PM
success.[^kivetsu-3-skillsets]
