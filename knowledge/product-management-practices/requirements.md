# Requirements

The traditional requirements document — a numbered list of "the system shall"
statements — is increasingly seen as an anti-pattern in modern product
management. The curated sources below trace the evolution from functional
specifications to a more outcome-oriented approach where the PM's job is to
describe the problem and the desired outcome, not to prescribe the solution.

Marty Cagan's three essays form the core of the modern critique: requirements
are not the answer, the engineering team needs a steady flow of well-prioritized
problems to solve ("feed the beast"), and the era of requirements as a
deliverable is ending. Joel Spolsky's classic on painless functional
specifications provides the historical foundation.

## From Specs to Outcomes

Joel Spolsky's "Painless functional specifications" is the classic defense of
writing things down before building — but in a lightweight, useful
way.[^spolsky-painless-functional-specs] Marty Cagan pushes further:
requirements are not the answer because they prescribe solutions rather than
describing problems.[^cagan-requirements-are-not] His "end of requirements"
essay argues that great product teams are given problems to solve, not
features to implement.[^cagan-end-of-requirements]

## Feeding the Beast

Cagan's "feed the beast" metaphor captures the PM's responsibility to keep the
engineering team supplied with a steady stream of well-prioritized, validated
problems — not a backlog of half-baked feature requests.[^cagan-feed-the-beast]
