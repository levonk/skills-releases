# Working with Cross-Functional Teams

The product manager does not build the product alone. The PM works at the
center of a triad — engineering, design, and product — and the quality of
those relationships determines the quality of the product. The curated sources
below cover how to work with each discipline, the common failure modes in
cross-functional collaboration, and how to lead a team when you have no
formal authority.

A recurring theme is the "Iceberg Secret": most of what makes a product work
is invisible to the people who use it, and most of what makes a team work is
invisible to the people outside it. The PM's job is to make the invisible
visible enough to align the team without drowning it in process.

## Working with Engineers

Nicholas Zakas's "care and feeding of software engineers" and Kenneth Norton's
"how to work with software engineers" both emphasize respecting engineers'
time, context, and expertise.[^zakas-care-and-feeding][^norton-how-to-work-with-engineers]
Julie Zhuo's "how to work with engineers" is a designer's perspective on the
PM-engineer relationship.[^zhuo-how-to-work-with-engineers] Ellen Chisa's
"engineers, your PM sucks" flips the perspective — advice for engineers stuck
with a bad PM.[^chisa-engineers-your-pm-sucks] Jeff Enderwick's "Nerds, get
the Product Management you deserve" is a call for engineers to demand better
PMs.[^enderwick-nerds-get-pm] Mark Stephan's "Top 5 Ways PMs can help
Developers Love them" is a practical checklist.[^stephan-top-5-ways-pm-help-devs]

## Working with Designers and PMs

Julie Zhuo contributes two complementary pieces: how to work with PMs (from a
designer's view) and how to work with designers (from a PM's
view).[^zhuo-how-to-work-with-pms][^zhuo-how-to-work-with-designers] These
paired essays are essential reading for anyone navigating the PM-designer
interface.

## Leading Cross-Functional Teams

Kenneth Norton's "leading cross-functional teams" is the canonical guide to
leading without authority.[^norton-leading-cross-functional] Joel Spolsky's
"Iceberg Secret, revealed" explains why stakeholders consistently
underestimate the work that goes into a product.[^spolsky-iceberg-secret]
Slava Akhmechet's "ten project management commandments" distills the
principles of running a project well.[^akhmechet-ten-pm-commandments] Eric
Ries's "product manager's lament" captures the structural tension PMs face
between authority and accountability.[^ries-pm-lament]
