# PM Books

Books remain the deepest format for building product management intuition.
Unlike blog posts or talks, a book forces an author to develop a complete
argument — the kind of sustained reasoning that changes how you think about
the role, not just what you do on a given day.

This reading list is grouped by domain. Start with the design and startups
sections if you are new to the field; the leadership section compounds over
time and rewards re-reading as your scope grows.

## Design

* **Inspired — How to Create Products Customers Love** — Marty Cagan. The canonical PM book on building products users love.
* **The Design of Everyday Things** — Donald Norman. The foundational text on user-centered design.
* **Don't Make Me Think** — Steve Krug. The pragmatic introduction to web usability.
* **About Face** — Alan Cooper et al. Comprehensive guide to interaction design.
* **The Elements of User Experience** — Jesse James Garrett. The layered model of UX that practitioners still reference.
* **Designing Products People Love** — How great designers create successful products.
* **Badass — Making Users Awesome** — Kathy Sierra. A behavior-driven approach to product design.

## Startups

* **The 4 Steps to the Epiphany** — Steve Blank. The origin of customer development methodology.
* **Business Model Generation** — A handbook for designing tomorrow's enterprises.
* **Beginning Customer Development** — Kevin Dewalt. A practical entry point into customer development.
* **The Innovator's Dilemma** — Clayton Christensen. Why good companies fail and how disruptive innovation works.
* **The Innovator's Solution** — Clayton Christensen. How companies can actively pursue disruption.
* **The Innovator's DNA** — Clayton Christensen. The skills that distinguish disruptive innovators.
* **The Hard Thing About Hard Things** — Ben Horowitz. The unglamorous reality of leading through crisis.
* **Traction** — Gabriel Weinberg. A framework for finding the right growth channel.
* **The Lean Product Playbook** — Dan Olsen. A step-by-step guide to achieving product-market fit.
* **Hooked — How to build habit forming products** — Nir Eyal. The Hook Model for building products that stick.
* **Crossing the Chasm** — Geoffrey A. Moore. The classic on taking products from early adopters to the mainstream.

## Marketing

* **Peopleware** — Tom DeMarco. The seminal book on managing teams of knowledge workers.
* **Understanding Media** — Marshall McLuhan. The extensions-of-man thesis that reframes how media shapes products.
* **Made to Stick** — Chip and Dan Heath. Why some ideas survive and others die.

## Product Development

* **Getting Real** — The smarter, faster, easier way to build a successful web application.
* **Joel on Software** — Joel Spolsky. Collected essays on software development and management.
* **Team Geek** — Brian Fitzpatrick. How to work effectively with engineers as a PM.
* **The Mythical Man-Month** — Frederick Brooks. Adding people to a late project makes it later — and other enduring lessons.
* **The Timeless Way of Building** — Christopher Alexander. The pattern-language philosophy that influenced software design.
* **Cracking the PM Interview** — Jackie Bavaro. The preparation guide for PM interviews.
* **The Art of Product Management** — Rich Mironov. Essays from a seasoned PM on the realities of the role.
* **Making It Right** — Rian Van Der Merwe. A holistic view of product management practice.
* **Product Leadership** — Richard Banfield, Martin Eriksson, Nate Walkingshaw. How top product leaders build great products and teams.
* **Lovability** — Brian de Haaff. A framework for building products people love.

## Leadership

* **How To Measure Anything** — Douglas W. Hubbard. A framework for quantifying seemingly intangible risks and values.
* **Creativity, Inc.** — Ed Catmull. The story of building a creative culture at Pixar.
* **Predictably Irrational** — Dan Ariely. The hidden forces that shape our decisions.
* **Freakonomics** — Steven Levitt and Stephen J. Dubwell. How incentives shape behavior in unexpected ways.
* **Getting Past NO** — William Ury. Negotiation as a core PM skill.
* **The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People** — Stephen R. Covey. The foundational personal-effectiveness framework.
* **Switch** — Chip and Dan Heath. How to change things when change is hard.
* **The Score Takes Care of Itself** — Bill Walsh. Leadership lessons from building a football dynasty.
* **Thinking, Fast and Slow** — Daniel Kahneman. The dual-system theory of decision-making.
* **Managing Humans** — Former Manager at Apple. Humorous and honest stories of managing technical teams.
* **The Everything Store** — Brad Stone. The story of Amazon and Jeff Bezos.
* **The Tipping Point** — Malcolm Gladwell. How small changes tip ideas into epidemics.
