# Becoming a Product Manager

There is no single path into product management. Practitioners arrive from
engineering, design, marketing, consulting, and even unrelated fields. What
unites the successful ones is a bias toward action — building something,
shipping something, or demonstrating product judgment before holding the title.

The curated sources below cover the practical question of how to break in,
whether a technical background is required, and how the skills expected of a PM
scale with seniority. The recurring advice: do a project, show product
thinking, and learn the craft by practicing it rather than waiting for
permission.

## Breaking In

Jackie Bavaro outlines four distinct paths into product management and argues
that a computer science background is helpful but not
required.[^bavaro-4-ways-to-break-in][^bavaro-cs-background] Ellen Chisa's
personal account of becoming a PM is paired with her concrete recommendation:
build a project to demonstrate product judgment.[^chisa-how-i-became-pm][^chisa-do-a-project]
Rohini Vibha's "So You Want to Manage a Product" is a field guide for
aspiring PMs navigating the transition.[^vibha-so-you-want-to-manage]

## Skills by Seniority

Brent Tworetzky's deep breakdown maps PM skills to seniority levels, showing
how the emphasis shifts from execution to strategy to leadership as you
grow.[^tworetzky-skills-by-seniority] Matt Schnitt's "4 mistakes new Product
Managers make" is a cautionary complement — the common failure modes of the
first months on the job.[^schnitt-4-mistakes]
