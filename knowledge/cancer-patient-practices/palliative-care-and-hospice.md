# Palliative Care and Hospice

## The Distinction That Matters

**Palliative care** = symptom and quality-of-life care available **at
any point** in a serious illness — it can start at diagnosis and runs
*alongside* curative treatment. You do not give up treatment to get
it.[^nia-palliative-hospice] Ask for a palliative referral early:
pain, nausea, fatigue, sleep, anxiety — symptom control is a specialty
and you shouldn't white-knuckle it.

**Hospice** = comfort-focused care when illness is no longer
responding to curative treatment or the patient chooses to stop it.
Eligibility requires a physician's certification that life expectancy
is **6 months or less** if the disease runs its normal
course.[^nci-hospice-pdq] Hospice is usually delivered at home and
covers the care team, medications for comfort, equipment, and family
support including bereavement.

## The Too-Late Pattern

Families routinely say they wish hospice had started sooner. Signals
it is time to have the conversation:

- Treatments are no longer working, or their burden outweighs their
  benefit
- Repeated hospitalizations for the same decline
- The patient values remaining time and comfort over further attempts
  at cure

Ask the oncologist the direct question: "Would you be surprised if I
were alive in a year?" — it cuts through prognostic vagueness better
than "how long do I have?"

## Resuscitation and Comfort Decisions

Whether resuscitation efforts should be made — CPR, ventilation,
ICU-level intervention — is an advance-directive question, and it is
**the patient's choice to make while they can make it**. A DNR order
is *not* required for hospice, though hospice patients who want "full
code" status need the goals-of-care conversation clarified.[^nci-hospice-pdq]
These documents and the reasoning around them live in the sibling
bundle — see
[Advance Directives and Resuscitation](https://github.com/levonk/skills-releases/blob/main/knowledge/estate-planning-practices/advance-directives-and-resuscitation.md).

## Common Mistakes

- **Conflating palliative with hospice.** Asking for palliative care
  at diagnosis is not "giving up" — it is symptom expertise running in
  parallel.
- **Treating hospice enrollment as defeat.** It is a service switch —
  comfort, equipment, and family support arriving at home.
- **Deferring the conversation.** Hospice's benefits (home nursing,
  respite, bereavement support) scale with time enrolled; the last
  days are the hardest time to arrange it.
