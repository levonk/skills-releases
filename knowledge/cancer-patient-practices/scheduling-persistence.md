# Scheduling Persistence

## The Core Practice

The scheduler's first answer is almost never the earliest possible
appointment — it is the earliest *default*. Persistence moves dates:

- **Call back.** If the appointment is weeks out, call again in a few
  days and ask whether anything sooner opened up. Ask specifically
  about cancellations. This routinely converts a 4-week wait into a
  same-week appointment.[^maxwell-cancer-2026]
- **Ask to be on the cancellation list.** Many offices keep one; you
  have to ask to be put on it.
- **Phone > portal for speed.** Portal messages queue behind clinical
  work. For anything time-sensitive, call.
- **Escalate gracefully.** If the front desk can't help, ask for the
  nurse line, the practice manager, or the referring physician's
  office to apply pressure from inside.

## The Follow-Up List

No one cares more about your case than you.[^maxwell-cancer-2026] Keep
a running list — in the same records folder as
[Medical Record Keeping](medical-record-keeping.md) — of every
promised action:

| Promised item | Who | Promised date | Follow-up date |
|---------------|-----|---------------|----------------|
| Referral to oncologist | Surgeon's office | — | +2 days |
| Genetic test order | Surgeon | this week | +3 days |
| Pathology slides to 2nd-opinion center | Hospital records | — | +2 days |

If the follow-up date arrives with no result, call. The seed source's
genetic-testing delay — months lost because a promised order was never
placed — is the canonical failure mode this list prevents.

## Delegating the Phone Work

Scheduling calls are the easiest task to hand to a spouse, adult
child, or friend — and in the seed case, a spouse's insistence on a
call-back produced the week-early ENT appointment.[^maxwell-cancer-2026]
Give the helper: provider names and numbers, insurance details, the
list of appointments to pull forward, and permission to be politely
relentless.

## Common Mistakes

- **Accepting the first offered date** for something urgent.
- **Assuming "we'll call you" means they will.** Put it on the
  follow-up list and call them.
- **Being apologetic about persistence.** Schedulers field these calls
  daily; polite insistence is normal, not rude.
