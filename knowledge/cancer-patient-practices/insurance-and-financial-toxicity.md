# Insurance and Financial Toxicity

"Financial toxicity" is the field's own term for what cancer costs do
to patients. The practices here keep money from silently narrowing
treatment options.

## Insurance Basics That Pay Off Early

- **Ask for a case manager.** Insurers can assign one; a named human
  beats the phone queue for the dozens of coverage questions
  ahead.[^cancercare-guide]
- **Read the policy now** — what requires pre-authorization,
  out-of-network rules, out-of-pocket maximum. Surprises come from
  unread fine print.
- **Verify second-opinion and travel coverage** before booking the
  cancer-center visit (see
  [Second Opinions and Cancer Centers](second-opinions-and-cancer-centers.md)).

## Appeals

You have the right to appeal any coverage denial — and a denied
appeal can be escalated to your state's insurance
commissioner.[^cancercare-guide] Keep every denial letter and appeal
in the records folder (see
[Medical Record Keeping](medical-record-keeping.md)). For
trial-related denials, the study coordinator can often supply the
supporting letter and journal citations insurers ask
for.[^nci-trials-paying]

## Assistance Channels

- **Patient navigator / social worker / financial counselor** — ask
  the care team for a referral on day one; they know which local and
  national programs you qualify for and can start applications
  early.[^acs-resources]
- **Charity care** — nonprofit and public hospitals maintain funds for
  patients who can't pay — **but you have to
  ask**.[^cancercare-guide] Payment plans are routine; ask before the
  bills pile up.
- **Co-pay relief programs** — disease-specific foundations (e.g.,
  Blood Cancer United for blood cancers) cover out-of-pocket
  treatment costs, transport, and lodging. Eligibility windows and
  waiting lists mean: **apply early**.[^acs-resources]
- **Disability benefits** — cancer qualifies as a disability under
  the ADA; SSI/SSDI may apply if you can't work. Employer disability
  insurance, FMLA leave, and state programs are separate channels —
  ask the navigator to map all of them.
- **Travel and lodging** — ACS and other organizations run lodging
  programs near major treatment centers; worth real money when the
  right care is a drive or flight away.

## Common Mistakes

- **Not disclosing financial strain to the care team.** Providers
  can't route you to help they don't know you need — raise it early.
- **Accepting first denials.** Appeals exist precisely because
  first-pass denials are common.
- **Waiting for the crisis.** Assistance programs have eligibility
  windows and waiting lists; early applications preserve options.
