# Professional Team and Advisors

Each cell below reads **Necessary / Recommended / Avoid**.

## The Tier Table

| Tier | Necessary | Recommended | Avoid |
|------|-----------|-------------|-------|
| 1 | Fee-only planner (one-time checkup) | — | Commissioned agents selling whole life; AUM advisors (nothing to manage yet) |
| 2 | CPA (tax return) | Fee-only planner (ongoing) | AUM charging ~1% on a small balance; whole-life/IBC (too early) |
| 3 | CPA; estate attorney (basic will package) | Fee-only planner | Bank "wealth advisors" cross-selling proprietary products |
| 4 | CPA/tax planner; estate attorney (trust) | CFP; P&C broker (umbrella) | AUM advisor who can't evaluate alternatives; compliance-only CPA |
| 5 | Tax planner; estate attorney; asset-protection attorney | CFP; IBC practitioner; P&C broker | Commission-based advisor; returns-only CPA |
| 6 | Tax strategist; estate attorney; bookkeeper; asset-protection attorney | IBC practitioner; MFO coach | "Fast-food AUM" advisors; IUL salespeople |
| 7 | Full MFO fractional team (tax, legal, bookkeeping, investments) | Trust officer (SD/NV); DAPT attorney | Generalists of any kind |
| 8 | Family office team (CIO, tax, legal, FO director) | International tax attorney; PTC architect | Anyone who can't show a written investment thesis and references |

## The Distinction That Matters Most

**A CPA is not a tax strategist.** Most CPAs file returns — compliance
work, looking backward. A tax strategist plans forward: entity
elections, timing, asset location, residency, charitable structures.
Nelson lists the gap between the two as the single most repeated
mistake across tiers 4–8 — the claimed delta for a real strategist
is on the order of tens of thousands per year at HNW
levels.[^nelson-mfo] When interviewing a "tax professional," ask what
they did for clients *between* filing deadlines, not during them.

## Advisor Compensation Models

- **Fee-only / flat / hourly** — paid by you, no product commissions.
  The default-safe model at every tier. NAPFA is the fee-only
  association.[^napfa]
- **AUM (% of assets under management)** — fine in principle at Tier
  4+ if the advisor can actually evaluate alternatives (real estate,
  private deals, IBC); a 1% AUM fee on a seven-figure index portfolio
  buys little.
- **Commission** — inherent conflict; every commissioned sale should
  be treated as a product pitch until proven otherwise. For insurance
  specifically, ask directly: *"How much are you getting paid on this
  policy, and how is it designed?"* — a well-designed IBC policy
  cuts the agent's commission dramatically (see
  [Infinite Banking and Family Banking](infinite-banking-and-family-bank.md)).

## Vetting Questions (any tier)

1. How are you compensated — all sources, including commissions and
   referral fees?
2. What do you do for clients outside filing/meeting season?
3. Show me a written plan/thesis you produced for a client like me.
4. What do you advise against, and when did you last tell a client
   not to buy something you sell?
5. Who are two clients I can call? (Tier 6+: references are
   non-negotiable.)

## The Fractional Team (Tiers 4+)

You do not need employees — you need a roster: tax strategist,
estate attorney, bookkeeper, P&C insurance broker, and (if pursuing
it) an IBC practitioner, coordinated on a monthly/quarterly/annual
cadence. This is the MFO model; see
[Micro Family Office](micro-family-office.md).

## Cautions

- **Advisor title inflation.** "Wealth manager," "private banker,"
  and "financial advisor" are marketing terms — the compensation
  model and the written plan are the signal, not the title.
- **Custody.** Assets should sit at a third-party custodian (the
  broker), never "with" the advisor — custody is the Madoff failure
  mode.
- **Free advice funnels.** MFO coaching programs and IBC
  practitioners monetize their frameworks; the frameworks are sound,
  but price the engagement before committing.
