# Infinite Banking and Family Banking

Infinite Banking (IBC, from Nelson Nash's *Becoming Your Own
Banker*) uses a **specially designed whole-life policy** as a private
liquidity system: you borrow against the policy's cash value instead
of a bank, repay yourself, and the cash value keeps compounding as if
the loan never left (the loan is collateralized by the policy, not
withdrawn from it).

## The Mechanics

- **Mutual insurance carriers only** — MassMutual, Penn Mutual and
  similar; policyholders own the company, and dividends accrue to
  policyholders.
- **PUA (Paid-Up Additions) rider** — the design trick: blend base
  premium with PUAs so cash value builds fast instead of death
  benefit. A well-designed policy cuts the agent's commission
  dramatically — which is why design is where the conflicts live.
- **MEC / 7-pay limit** — overfund past the IRS Modified Endowment
  Contract limits and the policy loses its tax treatment; design
  runs close to but under the line.
- **Policy loans** — no credit check, no fixed schedule, interest to
  the insurer; borrow ~85–90% of cash value while it keeps earning.
  Loans outstanding reduce the death benefit dollar-for-dollar.
- **The pitch vs reality** — "earn while you borrow" is real
  *mechanically* (loan interest ≈ credited rate leaves modest
  arbitrage or small deficit) but the early years run a deficit:
  cash value is below premiums paid for roughly years 1–3.

## The Family Banking System (multi-generational)

Jayson Lowe's extension: a **pool of policies across insured family
members** operated like a bank — credit checks for family loans,
attorney-ran closings, auto-debit repayments, a stated family loan
rate (Nash-model ~10%), a "one strike" default rule, an annual family
banking meeting, and new policies issued at adulthood rather than
gifted early. Reported example: dozens of policies across dozens of
insured lives over ~18 years. It is governance machinery more than
insurance — see
[Family Governance and Succession](family-governance-and-succession.md).

## When It Fits (and When It Doesn't)

- **Tier floor: Tier 4–5.** Below that, premiums crowd out the
  account-maxing that returns more (see
  [Foundation and Accumulation](foundation-and-accumulation.md)).
- **Best fit:** disciplined savers with stable surplus, business
  owners with real uses for recaptured interest (equipment, inventory,
  bridging), and families running a multi-generation governance
  system.
- **Not for:** anyone who would let premiums lapse (early surrender
  is the catastrophic loss case), anyone needing the money back
  inside ~5 years, or anyone treating it as their whole financial
  plan. Practitioners themselves warn against running *living
  expenses* through the policy — insurers cap premiums relative to
  income, and consumption loans have no recapture stream.

## The Vetting Checklist (practitioner selection)

1. **"How much are you getting paid on this policy, and how is it
   designed?"** — demand the commission answer; PUA-heavy designs
   reduce agent commission by 60–90%. A $20k-premium policy paying
  ~$3–5k commission is plausible; the same premium paying $20k+ is a
   misdesign.
2. **75–90% first-year cash access** — ask what fraction of year-1
   premium is available as cash value.
3. **Mutual carrier, dividend history** — decades of uninterrupted
   dividends.
4. **Term blend available** — keep real death-benefit coverage cheap
   alongside the banking design.

## The Honest Criticism List

- The source material is advocacy — its authors sell policies,
  policies designs, or coaching; treat return claims as marketing.
- Headline "loss" stories ("cost me $10M") turn out to be foregone
  *commissions* reframed as practitioner-alignment proof.
- **IUL ≠ IBC.** Indexed-universal-life "laser fund" pitches
  (0%-floor, 8–10% claimed crediting) are a different product that
  IBC practitioners themselves criticize for lapse risk — both
  factions can't be right, and both are selling.
- Policy loans are a *liquidity* tool, not yield — total policy
  returns approximate high-grade bond returns over decades, not
  equity returns.
- 1035-exchanging an underperforming policy into a new one can
  crystallize losses and reset surrender schedules.

## Cautions

- Never surrender in the deficit years; never let premiums lapse.
- The strategy's safety claims (state guaranty funds, mutual-carrier
  stability) are genuine but are *insurer-credit* claims — still a
  concentrated counterparty.
- Model "policy vs taxable brokerage" on your own numbers with a
  fee-only planner before committing; the Venn overlap where IBC wins
  is real but narrower than its marketing.
