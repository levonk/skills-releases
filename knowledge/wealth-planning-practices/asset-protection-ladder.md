# Asset Protection Ladder

Protection is a **ladder climbed in order** — each rung is more
expensive and less reversible than the last. Most people should stop
at rung 2; almost nobody needs rung 5.

## The Ladder

1. **Insurance (all tiers, cheapest rung)** — liability coverage,
   umbrella ≈ net worth, entities named as additional insureds. See
   [Insurance and Protection Levels](insurance-and-protection-levels.md).
2. **Statutory exemptions (free)** — ERISA-qualified retirement
   accounts have near-absolute federal creditor protection; most IRAs
   get state-level protection (check your state — it varies);
   **homestead exemptions** protect some or all home equity in states
   like FL and TX. Know your state's exemptions before building
   anything.
3. **Entities and titling (~Tier 3–5)** — each risky asset (rental,
   business) in its own LLC so a claim can't reach across;
   charging-order states (NV/WY) limit a personal creditor to a lien
   on distributions; **tenancy-by-entirety** titling protects marital
   assets from one spouse's creditors where available. See
   [Entity Structuring and Domicile](entity-structuring-and-domicile.md).
4. **Domestic asset protection trusts (~Tier 6–7)** — self-settled
   trusts in DAPT states (SD/NV/WY/AK and others): you can be a
   beneficiary of your own trust after a statutory waiting period.
   See [Residency and Domestic Havens](residency-and-domestic-havens.md).
5. **Offshore trusts (~Tier 7–8, rare)** — Cook Islands / Nevis:
   foreign courts don't recognize US judgments, forcing creditors to
   re-litigate overseas. Expensive (five figures to set up, annual
   maintenance), fully reportable (FBAR/FATCA), and only sensible for
   the highest-risk profiles. See
   [International Structures](international-structures.md).

## The Rules That Bind Every Rung

- **Fraudulent transfer law overrides everything.** Moving assets
  after a claim exists (or is foreseeable) can be unwound and can
  convert a civil problem into a worse one. Asset protection is
  built *before* the claim — that's why it's a ladder you climb as
  net worth grows, not a tool you grab when sued.
- **Protection is about negotiation posture, not invisibility.** The
  practical goal is making a claim expensive and uncertain enough
  that creditors settle for the insurance money.
- **Separate the risk from the gold.** Operations that can be sued
  (rentals, businesses) live in OpCos; assets you keep (equity,
  cash) live up-stack in holding entities or trusts.

## Cautions

- **DAPTs are state-law dependent** — contested in bankruptcy and
  against certain creditor classes (e.g., some family-law claims);
  the grantor-beneficiary feature is newer law, not settled law.
- **Offshore is compliance-first** — FBAR, FATCA/Form 8938, and
  worldwide US taxation apply; the structure adds cost and filing
  burden, not secrecy.
- **Don't confuse asset protection with hiding.** UBO disclosure,
  discovery, and fraudulent-transfer statutes all reach through
  "anonymous" structures.
