# Micro Family Office

The response to the **financial-services desert** — too wealthy for
cookie-cutter advisors ($1M+), below the single-family-office
threshold (~$100M). The MFO is not an institution; it's an operating
system: *run your wealth like a business* — CEO mindset, fractional
executives, written policies, a meeting cadence.[^nelson-mfo]

## The Four Phases (WealthOps)

1. **Architect** — write the three founding documents:
   - **Legacy statement** — what the wealth is *for* (freedom,
     family, philanthropy); every later decision checks against it.
   - **Investment thesis** — asset classes you will and won't own,
     sizing rules, target returns, deal-sourcing discipline.
   - **Honest assessment** — net-worth statement, income sources,
     entity inventory, document inventory, advisor inventory — the
     "books" of the business.
2. **Build** — assemble the **fractional team**: tax strategist,
   estate attorney, bookkeeper, P&C broker, investment counsel, and
   specialists as needed (IBC practitioner, asset-protection
   attorney). Pay for hours and outcomes, not AUM.
3. **Run** — the operating cadence:
   - **Monthly**: bookkeeping close, cash-flow report.
   - **Quarterly**: portfolio review against the written thesis —
     not against the market's mood.
   - **Annually**: estate-document review, insurance re-leveling,
     team performance review.
   - Target: **~5–10 hours/week** of CEO-of-wealth attention once
     built.
4. **Succession** — heir education, governance docs, successor
   trustees/executors named and briefed — see
   [Family Governance and Succession](family-governance-and-succession.md).

## The Six Mistakes the Framework Exists to Kill

1. Random investing without a written thesis.
2. Wrong advisors (product salespeople, compliance-only CPAs).
3. Thinking about taxes only at filing time — planning is year-round.
4. Oversizing deals (cap ~10% of net worth per private deal).
5. Managing millions like a savings account (no cadence, no books).
6. Neglecting succession until it's a crisis.

## Ops Manual

The MFO writes its procedures down: who approves what, where documents
live, which accounts pay which entities, the cadence calendar, and the
succession memo (who to call, what breaks, backup attorney) — the
document that prevents a single point of failure in a family that runs
like a business.

## Cautions

- **The framework is workshop-gated** — the public material teaches
  the phases well and the execution thinly; paid coaching is the
  funnel. You can build the same roster à la carte.
- **Fractional ≠ free.** A real fractional team costs thousands per
  month at Tier 6; the business case is that a tax strategist and
  proper books return multiples of that — verify on your own numbers.
- **Cadence beats structure.** An MFO that isn't run (no monthly
  close, no quarterly review) is just a pile of LLCs.
