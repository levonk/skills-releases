# Business Acquisition and Income

The wealth roadmap's spending side is tiers; its *income* side is
this page. At Tiers 1–3, the highest-leverage move is often not
frugal optimization but **owning a cash-flowing small business**.

## Boring-Business Acquisition (the playbook)

The model associated with Codie Sanchez's *Main Street Millionaire*
canon: buy an unglamorous, already-profitable, owner-financed small
business (laundromats, car washes, vending, trades, cleaning) instead
of starting one.

- **The funnel** — the ratio that matters: roughly 100 candidates →
  50 conversations → 10 serious → 1 deal. Plan for months, not weeks.
- **Motivated sellers** — the "Seven D's": death, divorce, disease,
  distress, dispute, departure (retirement without a successor),
  debt. Retiring baby-boomer owners are the structural seller wave.
- **Deal feeders** — the "Four A's" who see sellers first:
  accountants, attorneys, insurance agents, advisors. Tell them what
  you're looking for.
- **SOWS filter** — pick businesses that are **S**table, **O**ld,
  **W**eak-marketed, **S**imple — decades-old demand, weak
  competition, simple operations you can improve.
- **Seller financing is the unlock** — the retiring owner carries
  much of the note; it aligns incentives and is the "no money" path
  that makes the math work at Tier 1–2 balance sheets.

## Real-Estate Reality Check

Two honest counterweights against the hype:

- **The Graham Stephan audit** — a self-reported seven-rental
  portfolio bought at ~3% rates returned ~4–5% true annualized after
  repairs, rent control, depreciation recapture, and hassle —
  competitive with index funds, not obviously better, and far more
  work. Real estate is a part-time business wearing an investment's
  clothes.
- **Structuring over selecting** — when real estate *is* right, the
  structure (per-property LLC under a holding company, §121 residence
  handling) matters as much as the deal; see
  [Entity Structuring and Domicile](entity-structuring-and-domicile.md).

## Raw-Land Due Diligence

Land is a different asset — no income, but cheap optionality. The
due-diligence frameworks:

- **ASSESS** — Access, Soil, Slope, Environmental, Street view,
  Satellite view.
- **The kill list** — landlocked parcels or easement gaps ("a road is
  only as good as its paperwork"), poor soil (dominant value driver),
  boundary disputes, heirship tangles, false/unrecorded easements,
  zoning traps, wetlands, HOA rules, hostile neighbors. Any one is a
  walk-away.

## "Passive Income," Calibrated

The reality ranking inside the corpus: dividend/REIT/HYSA income is
real but slow; P2P lending and crowdfunded real estate carry platform
risk; digital products, affiliate, and rental (Turo/Neighbor-type)
income are businesses with marketing attached; royalties are real but
narrow. **Cost elimination is the only guaranteed passive income** —
every recurring expense cut is a riskless after-tax yield.

## Cautions

- **The corpus caveat** — most of this material is influencer
  content; income figures ("$58k/month tree removal") are anecdotes
  for marketing, not data. Use the frameworks, verify the numbers.
- **A small business is a job first** — the "absentee owner" version
  is largely myth; budget operator time or a real manager.
- **Due diligence kills** — the funnel exists because most businesses
  for sale shouldn't be bought; don't fall in love at deal 40.
