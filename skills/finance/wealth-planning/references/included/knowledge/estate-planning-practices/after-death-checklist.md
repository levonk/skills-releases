# After-Death Checklist

Written for the executor/family, and best left for them in the
[letter of instruction](documentation-and-letter-of-instruction.md).
Order matters: body first, certificates early, everything else through
the certificates.

## Immediate (first 24–48 hours)

- **If under hospice**: call the hospice line — they send a nurse to
  pronounce death and coordinate the disposition provider. Do not
  call 911 unless the plan says to (a 911 call can trigger unwanted
  resuscitation — the POLST/DNR visible at home prevents this; see
  [Advance Directives and Resuscitation](advance-directives-and-resuscitation.md)).
- **If not under hospice**: call the disposition provider directly —
  e.g., Neptune Society's one-call service sends personnel to
  transport the deceased and notify the family members the deceased
  designated.[^neptune-faqs]
- **Notify** the inner circle: healthcare proxy, POA agents,
  executor, close family.
- **Secure** the home, vehicle, pets, and perishables.

## First Week

- **Order certified death certificates** — 10–20 copies is the common
  recommendation; every institution wants an original and returns it
  slowly. Order through the disposition provider (they procure them;
  ~$26 each in CA) — far easier than ordering later from the
  county.[^neptune-central-ca]
- **Locate** the will, trust, letter of instruction, and any pre-need
  disposition contract.
- **Meet the attorney** — probate filing and executor appointment if
  required; trust administration if a trust exists (see
  [Core Estate Documents](core-estate-documents.md)).
- **Start the notifications list** (below).

## Notifications (weeks 1–4, each needs a certified certificate)

- **Social Security** — report the death; a one-time payment and
  survivor benefits may apply (funeral homes often file this; verify)
- **Employer/pension** — final pay, retirement survivor benefits
- **Life insurers** — each policy; the fastest big-money item
- **Banks/brokerages** — retitle or transfer per beneficiary
  designations (see
  [Digital Estate and Beneficiaries](digital-estate-and-beneficiaries.md))
- **Credit cards** — notify issuers; stop autopay subscriptions
- **VA** — burial benefits, survivor pension if veteran (DD-214
  required)
- **Utilities, subscriptions, memberships** — cancel or transfer
- **Tax preparer** — final income-tax return and estate returns

## Probate Basics

- Assets with beneficiary designations, joint ownership, or held in a
  trust pass **outside probate**.
- Assets held in the deceased's name alone go through probate —
  court-supervised distribution; the will names the executor who runs
  it.
- Probate is public and takes months; this is why the
  [living trust](core-estate-documents.md) and beneficiary
  designations exist.

## Common Mistakes

- **Ordering too few certificates.** Reorder trips to the county
  clerk are the classic delay.
- **Notifying before securing.** Property and financial accounts
  should be secured first.
- **Paying the deceased's debts personally.** Debts are paid by the
  estate, in a defined order — the executor should not write personal
  checks.
- **Rushing distributions.** Creditors' claims periods must run
  first; premature distribution can make the executor personally
  liable.
