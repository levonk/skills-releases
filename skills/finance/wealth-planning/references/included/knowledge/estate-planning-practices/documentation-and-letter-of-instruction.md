# Documentation and Letter of Instruction

## The Problem This Solves

Estate documents answer "what should happen." The letter of
instruction answers "how do you actually do it" — the informal,
non-legal document that tells the executor and family where
everything is and what to do first. Estates stall for months over
missing account lists and unfound keys, not legal complexity.

## The Master Inventory

One document (or a folder with an index) containing:

**Document locations**
- Will and trust — where the *originals* live (attorney, safe,
  county filing), not just copies
- POAs, advance directive, POLST/DNR — original locations and who's
  holding copies
- Deeds, titles, birth/marriage certificates, military discharge
  (DD-214 — needed for VA benefits), insurance policies
- Safe-deposit box location **and who has access** (boxes seal at
  death; plan for it)

**People to call** (with phone numbers)
- Attorney, accountant/tax preparer, financial advisor
- Executor, successor trustee, POA agents, healthcare proxy
- Employer HR, insurance agents

**Accounts and debts** — the inventory from
[Digital Estate and Beneficiaries](digital-estate-and-beneficiaries.md):
institutions, account numbers, contact paths.

**Disposition wishes**
- Chosen provider (e.g., Neptune Society) and its phone number,
  whether a pre-need contract exists and its contract number — see
  [Body Disposition](body-disposition.md)
- Burial/cremation/donation preference, cemetery plot deed if owned,
  service wishes, obituary draft or notes

**Digital access**
- Password manager emergency-access setup, master password location,
  phone passcode — per
  [Digital Estate and Beneficiaries](digital-estate-and-beneficiaries.md)

## Storage and Disclosure

- Keep the letter with the will — a fireproof safe or the attorney's
  file; tell the executor, spouse, and healthcare proxy it exists and
  where.
- It is **not a legal document** — freely amendable, no notarization;
  date each revision.
- Review annually or on any life change; staleness is the main
  failure mode.

## Common Mistakes

- **Inventory exists but is unfindable.** The plan that can't be
  found doesn't exist.
- **Listing accounts without access paths.** "Brokerage at Fidelity"
  without the account number and the death-claims phone number still
  costs the family weeks.
- **Keeping the letter secret until death.** The executor and proxy
  should read it *now* — questions are answerable while the author
  can answer them.
