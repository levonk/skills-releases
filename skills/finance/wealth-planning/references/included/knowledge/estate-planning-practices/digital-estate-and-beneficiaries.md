# Digital Estate and Beneficiaries

## Beneficiary Designations Override the Will

Retirement accounts (401k, IRA), life insurance, and payable-on-death
bank/brokerage accounts transfer to **whoever the designation form
names** — regardless of what the will or trust says.[^kiplinger-docs]
Outdated designations (an ex-spouse, a deceased parent, a blank field)
are one of the most common and most damaging estate failures.

**Action:** pull every account's beneficiary record, update to match
current intent, and record the confirmation dates in the inventory —
see
[Documentation and Letter of Instruction](documentation-and-letter-of-instruction.md).

## The Digital Asset Inventory

The family needs a list of what exists before they can handle it:

- **Financial**: banks, brokerages, retirement accounts, pensions,
  crypto wallets/exchanges (seed phrase location!), HSAs
- **Income streams**: employer HR/payroll contact, pension
  administrator, Social Security, annuity providers
- **Debts**: mortgage servicer, credit cards, auto loans
- **Insurance**: life, health, home, auto, long-term care — policy
  numbers and agent contacts
- **Digital life**: email accounts (the master key — password resets
  flow there), cloud storage, photo libraries, social accounts,
  domains, subscriptions
- **Loyalty/benefits**: airline miles, points programs

## Password Manager Emergency Access

The single most valuable digital-estate move: **put everything in a
password manager and configure its emergency-access feature** for the
trusted agent/family member. 1Password, Bitwarden, and similar tools
support legacy/emergency contacts with a waiting period. Absent that,
the master password's location goes in the letter of instruction — a
sealed envelope with the attorney or in the safe, never in email.

## Platform Legacies

- **Email** (the linchpin): Apple/Google account-recovery contacts and
  legacy-contact features
- **Social**: Facebook legacy contact / memorialization, others'
  deletion-or-memorialize options
- **Phone**: the device passcode — two-factor codes and photo
  libraries live behind it; record it in the letter of instruction

## Common Mistakes

- **Letting designations drift.** Review after any life event:
  marriage, divorce, birth, death of a beneficiary.
- **No crypto plan.** Self-custody keys with no recovery path = the
  asset dies with the holder.
- **Subscriptions on autopay.** An inventory lets the executor cancel
  them in week one instead of discovering them on statements for a
  year.
