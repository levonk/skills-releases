# Charitable Giving Structures

Charitable structures sit on the tax ladder at rung 4 — after
accounts, asset location, and harvesting are handled. The lever they
all share: **donate appreciated assets, not cash** — you deduct fair
market value and never realize the capital gain.

## The Three Structures

### Donor-Advised Fund (Tier 4+)

A DAF is a charitable account at a sponsor (Fidelity Charitable,
Schwab Charitable, Vanguard Charitable).[^fidelity-charitable]
Contribute appreciated stock → **immediate full deduction** →
recommend grants to charities over time.

- **Bunching** — contribute 2–3 years of giving in one year to clear
  the standard-deduction threshold, then grant out annually.
- **Anonymity** — grants can be made anonymously; useful for
  windfall/public visibility situations.
- **Successor advisors** — children can inherit the advisory role; a
  lightweight family-philanthropy practice without a foundation.
- **The catch** — contributions are irrevocable and grants are
  "recommendations" the sponsor can technically decline (rarely
  exercised, but the control you keep is advisory, not ownership).

### Private Foundation (Tier 7–8)

Your own charitable corporation: full control, family employment,
named legacy — and the most overhead: annual filings are public, the
**5% minimum annual distribution** rule applies, and legal/CPA costs
are real. The math favors a foundation over a DAF only at
multi-million-dollar scale or when control/legacy is the point.

### Charitable Trusts (Tier 7+)

- **CLT (Charitable Lead Trust)** — charity receives an annuity for a
  term; heirs receive the remainder at reduced gift-tax cost. CLAT
  (fixed annuity) vs CLUT (revalued). See
  [Advanced Estate Instruments](advanced-estate-instruments.md).
- **CRT (Charitable Remainder Trust)** — inverts it: you keep an
  income stream, charity gets the remainder. Useful for
  diversifying a concentrated appreciated position without a
  one-time gain.

## Which When

| Situation | Structure |
|-----------|-----------|
| Want the deduction now, grants later, no overhead | DAF |
| Annual giving below ~$100k, value simplicity | DAF |
| Family employment + named legacy + $5M+ committed | Private foundation |
| Income stream + eventual gift + concentrated position | CRT |
| Transfer-appreciation-to-heirs play with a charitable kicker | CLAT |

## Cautions

- **DAF contributions are gone.** Advisory control only — no personal
  benefit, no getting it back, and quid-pro-quo grants (e.g., paying
  gala tables that include your seat) are prohibited.
- **Foundations are public.** Form 990-PF is a public record —
  grants, salaries, and assets are visible.
- **Charitable intent is load-bearing.** These are deduction vehicles
  for money you actually intend to give; the tax benefit is the
  discount on giving, not a profit.

[^fidelity-charitable]: Fidelity Charitable — the largest DAF
    sponsor; Schwab and Vanguard Charitable are comparable.
