# Advanced Estate Instruments

The core documents (will, revocable trust, POAs, directives) are the
floor — they're in
[estate-planning-practices](../estate-planning-practices/index.md) and
required from Tier 1. This page is the *transfer-tax toolbox* that
turns on at Tier 5+ when the estate-tax exemption is at risk.

## The Problem These Solve

The federal estate tax takes ~40% of the taxable estate above the
exemption. The exemption is large (~$13.61M/person in 2024, ~$15M
under the 2025 OBBBA schedule) **but has sunset before** (it was
legislated to roughly halve) — Tier 5–7 planning is mostly about
locking in today's exemption by moving appreciation out of the estate
*before* it appreciates further.

## The Instruments

- **ILIT (Irrevocable Life Insurance Trust)** — the trust owns the
  life policy; the death benefit pays out *outside* the estate,
  giving heirs tax-free liquidity to pay estate tax without fire
  sales. Trust must be both beneficiary **and** owner — naming the
  trust only as beneficiary can leave the proceeds in the taxable
  estate.
- **GRAT (Grantor Retained Annuity Trust)** — you gift appreciating
  assets, keep an annuity stream that "zeroes out" the taxable gift,
  and everything above the §7520 hurdle rate passes to heirs
  tax-free. Risk: die during the term and the assets revert to the
  estate — so they're run short (2–3 years) and *rolled*.
- **SLAT (Spousal Lifetime Access Trust)** — irrevocable trust for the
  spouse's benefit; moves assets out while a couple keeps indirect
  access through the spouse. Both spouses creating SLATs for each
  other requires care (reciprocal-trust doctrine).
- **IDGT (Intentionally Defective Grantor Trust)** — sell appreciating
  assets to the trust for a note; the sale isn't an income-tax event
  (grantor trust) but the assets are outside the estate; the grantor
  paying the trust's income tax is itself a tax-free gift.
- **CLT (Charitable Lead Trust)** — charity gets an annuity for a
  term, heirs get the remainder: CLAT (fixed) vs CLUT (revalued
  annually). Strong when §7520 rates are low. See
  [Charitable Giving Structures](charitable-giving-structures.md).
- **FLP (Family Limited Partnership)** — consolidate family assets;
  gift limited-partner interests at *valuation discounts*
  (illiquidity, minority stake) to move more per dollar of exemption.
- **Dynasty trust** — irrevocable trust in a perpetual-duration state
  (SD; NV ~365 years) holding assets for generations, skipping estate
  tax at each generational hand-off. The apex structure at Tier 7–8;
  see
  [Residency and Domestic Havens](residency-and-domestic-havens.md)
  and
  [Family Governance and Succession](family-governance-and-succession.md).

## Sequencing

1. Fund the revocable trust and fix beneficiaries (Tier 4) — basics
   first.
2. ILIT once life insurance is in the picture and the estate could
   plausibly exceed the exemption (Tier 5).
3. Gift appreciating assets into GRAT/SLAT/IDGT while the exemption
   is high (Tier 5–7 — this is the sunset hedge).
4. Dynasty trust + governance layer at Tier 7+ when the question is
   generations, not retirement.

## Cautions

- **All irrevocable.** These instruments trade control for tax
  efficiency; once assets go in, the terms govern. Model the
  "I need it back" scenario before funding.
- **Exemption figures are moving targets.** Every dollar figure here
  is dated — confirm the current exemption and sunset schedule before
  planning around it.
- **Step-up works the other way.** Assets held to death get a basis
  reset — sometimes *not* gifting is the tax-smart move; the math is
  per-asset.
