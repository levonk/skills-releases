# Residency and Domestic Havens

Two separate tools get conflated: **where you live** (personal
domicile → state income tax) and **where your trust lives** (trust
situs → taxation + duration + protection).

## Strategic Residency (personal domicile)

The no-income-tax states for individuals: **FL, TX, NV, WY, SD, AK,
WA** (plus TN/NH with narrow exceptions). Worth real money at high
income — a CA or NY earner can save 5–13% of income — but domicile is
a *facts* test, not a mailing-address test.

**The domicile-proof checklist:**

- Physically present in the new state **183+ days** (keep a day-count
  log — phone records and card statements get subpoenaed)
- Driver's license, voter registration, vehicle registration
- Primary doctors/dentists, religious/community membership, kids'
  school
- Estate documents re-executed under new-state law
- File a part-year return the move year; expect the old state to
  audit if the sum is large (CA and NY are aggressive)

For expats, the FEIE (~$126k+, indexed) excludes foreign earned
income but changes nothing about investment income or self-employment
tax; **renunciation** of citizenship is the only full severance and
carries an exit tax plus permanent consequences — an extreme edge
case, not a planning tool.

## Domestic Trust Havens (trust situs)

Irrevocable trusts are sited where the trust law is best, not where
you live:

- **South Dakota** — the benchmark: perpetual dynasty trusts, no
  state income tax on trust accumulation, top-tier directed-trust and
  privacy statutes.
- **Nevada** — 365-year dynasty duration, strong creditor protection,
  two-year DAPT seasoning.
- **Wyoming, Alaska** — strong DAPT statutes, competitive
  directed-trust law.
- **Tennessee, Delaware** — credible alternatives; DE is more
  corporate than personal-trust focused.

### DAPT (Domestic Asset Protection Trust)

A **self-settled** trust — you transfer assets in and can remain a
*permissible* beneficiary — that becomes creditor-resistant after a
statutory waiting period (~2–4 years by state). Key mechanics:
independent trustee required, discretionary distributions only, and
the waiting period means DAPTs must be built **before** trouble.

### Quiet and Directed Trusts

- **Quiet trust** — the trustee is *not required* to notify
  beneficiaries for a period; used so heirs don't plan their lives
  around the inheritance.
- **Directed trust** — splits the trustee role: an administrative
  trustee (filings, custody), an investment advisor (allocations),
  and a distribution committee (who gets what). Lets a family keep
  investment control while getting the haven state's law.

## Cautions

- **DAPTs are unsettled against all creditor types.** Bankruptcy
  courts and family-law claims have reached self-settled trusts;
  treat a DAPT as a strong deterrent, not a vault.
- **Your home state's tax follows you until domicile truly moves.** A
  WY trust doesn't shelter a CA resident's CA-source income.
- **Situs shopping has fees** — professional trustees in haven states
  charge annually; below ~$5M of trust assets the cost-benefit often
  fails.
