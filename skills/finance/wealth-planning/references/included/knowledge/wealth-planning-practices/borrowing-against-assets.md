# Borrowing Against Assets

At Tier 4+, the liquidity question inverts: instead of "what do I
sell," it becomes "what do I borrow against" — because selling
appreciated assets realizes tax and interrupts compounding.

## The Instruments

- **Mortgage** — cheapest long-term secured money; fixed rate,
  amortizing, no mark-to-market call risk. First lien on a residence
  preserves §121 treatment.
- **SBLOC (Securities-Backed Line of Credit)** — borrow ~30–70% of a
  pledged taxable portfolio at broker rates; technically a
  *non-purpose* loan (cannot buy more securities), variable rate,
  callable — the broker can force repayment on a drawdown.
- **Margin** — ~50% advance (Reg T), technically for buying
  securities, same maintenance-call mechanics, usually a few points
  more expensive than an SBLOC.
- **Policy loans** — against whole-life cash value; see
  [Infinite Banking and Family Banking](infinite-banking-and-family-bank.md).
- **HELOC / First-lien HELOC** — home equity; cheap standby liquidity
  but lines can be **frozen in a downturn** (2008 precedent), rates
  are variable, and interest-only drift converts it to perpetual
  debt. Useful opened *before* you need it, sized well under value,
  never as an income substitute.

## Interest-Tracing Rules

Deductibility follows **use**: mortgage interest (acquisition debt),
investment interest (margin used for taxable investments), business
interest (loans to an entity). SBLOC "non-purpose" interest is
generally *not* deductible — the same dollar borrowed has different
after-tax cost depending on the instrument. Label the use at
origination.

## The Playbook (home purchase, Tier 4+)

Three patterns from practice: **SBLOC → refi into mortgage** once the
purchase clears; **mortgage + SBLOC bridge** for speed; or
**perpetual SBLOC** for those deliberately running the
borrow-don't-sell thesis. Each needs a worked cost model
(rate×advance-rate×term) before picking.

## Safety Engineering

- **Segregate pledged from lifestyle accounts** — never pledge
  retirement accounts (also often prohibited); keep a clean
  unpledged pool for living expenses.
- **Refuse cross-collateralization** — read the pledge agreement;
  one facility should not drag down unrelated accounts.
- **Hedge the pledged pool** — protective puts, collars, or keeping
  12–24 months of debt service in cash/T-bills against a
  maintenance-call scenario.
- **Match insurance to the loan** — death or incapacity with a
  callable loan outstanding is the ugly case; term coverage and POA
  language covering the facility are cheap fixes (see
  [estate-planning-practices](../estate-planning-practices/index.md)).
- **Internal alert thresholds + annual review** — treat loan-to-value
  like a bank would; decide the deleveraging trigger in advance.

## Where Buy-Borrow-Die Becomes Rational

Buy-borrow-die — hold appreciating assets, borrow against them for
spending, let the basis step up at death — is a **Tier 5+** play:
it needs a large enough asset base for facility pricing, enough
appreciation that deferral beats the interest cost, and estate
planning that makes the step-up land. Below that it is leverage for
consumption, which is the thing it pretends not to be. See
[Tax Optimization Ladder](tax-optimization-ladder.md).

## Cautions

- **Variable rates + callability** — every instrument above except
  fixed mortgages can be repriced or called against you exactly when
  markets fall; size for the call, not the plan.
- **"Equity earns 0%"** marketing (equity-strip/IUL pitches) mixes a
  true observation (dead home equity) with a product conclusion that
  doesn't follow — the alternative to dead equity is not
  automatically an insurance product.
- **Velocity banking is debt management, not wealth building** — it
  can accelerate payoff for the disciplined, but it manufactures no
  return.
