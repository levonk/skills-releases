# Insurance and Protection Levels

Insurance is the **first rung of the asset-protection ladder** — it
is bought before entities and trusts because it is the cheapest
protection per dollar (see
[Asset Protection Ladder](asset-protection-ladder.md)).

## The Tier Table

| Tier | Health | Auto/Home | Umbrella | Life | Disability | Long-Term Care |
|------|--------|-----------|----------|------|------------|----------------|
| 1 | Adequate HDHP | State min + liability | — | Term (income replacement) | If W-2, yes | — |
| 2 | HDHP + HSA | Adequate liability | — | Term (10–20× income) | Yes | — |
| 3 | HDHP/PPO | High liability limits | $1M | Term | Yes | Consider |
| 4 | PPO | High limits | $2M–$5M | Term + first whole-life (IBC seed) | Yes | Yes |
| 5 | PPO | High limits | $5M–$10M (≈ NW) | IBC whole-life (high early cash value) | Declining need | Yes |
| 6 | PPO | High limits | $10M+ / entity-level | IBC + ILIT-owned policies | Portfolio-backed | Yes |
| 7 | PPO | Entity-level | $10M+ / DAPT-backed | ILIT + family banking system | Portfolio-backed | Yes |
| 8 | PPO | Entity-level | DAPT/offshore-backed | ILIT + dynasty trust-owned | Portfolio-backed | Funded via trust |

## The Umbrella Rule

**Umbrella coverage ≈ net worth**, and **name every LLC as an
additional insured**. Umbrella is the cheapest lawsuit protection in
existence — $1M of coverage typically costs a few hundred dollars a
year. Below-net-worth umbrella coverage is the most common protection
gap in the $500k–$5M band. Buy it before entities, not after.

## Term vs. Permanent Life

- **Term life** — pure income replacement; the right answer from
  Tier 1 through mid-Tier 4. Buy 10–20× income while young and
  healthy.
- **Permanent (whole life, IBC-designed)** — becomes interesting at
  Tier 4–5 as a *banking asset*, not as insurance. High-early-cash-value
  mutual-company designs only; see
  [Infinite Banking and Family Banking](infinite-banking-and-family-bank.md)
  for mechanics and the criticism list.
- **ILIT-owned policies** — at Tier 5+, a life policy owned by an
  irrevocable life insurance trust keeps the death benefit outside
  the taxable estate and provides estate liquidity (see
  [Advanced Estate Instruments](advanced-estate-instruments.md)).

## Disability and Long-Term Care

- **Disability** — the most under-owned coverage at Tiers 1–4; your
  income is the asset being protected. Own-occupation, to-65 coverage
  for W-2 earners; becomes less necessary as the portfolio can
  self-insure (~Tier 5–6).
- **Long-term care** — evaluate around Tier 3–4 in your 50s; at
  Tier 6+ most families self-fund or fund through the trust rather
  than buy standalone LTC policies.

## Health Insurance Note

At Tier 1–2 an HDHP paired with an HSA is usually optimal — the HSA
is the only triple-tax-advantaged account (deductible in, tax-free
growth, tax-free out for medical) and doubles as a stealth retirement
account. Move to PPO when family/predictable-care economics favor it.

## Cautions

- **Insurance is sold, not bought.** Every agent conversation starts
  with "how are you paid?" Commission-driven designs inflate the
  death benefit and shrink early cash value.
- **Umbrella gaps on entities.** If the umbrella doesn't name the
  LLCs, the protection ladder has a hole at the bottom rung.
- **Over-insuring late tiers.** At Tier 7–8, entity structures and
  trusts replace retail insurance for liability; keep P&C coverage
  entity-level, not personal.
