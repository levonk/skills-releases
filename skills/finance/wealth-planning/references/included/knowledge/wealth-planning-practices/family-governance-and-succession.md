# Family Governance and Succession

The statistic that drives this page: **most wealthy families lose the
wealth by generation two**, and nearly all by generation three — not
to taxes or markets, but to unprepared heirs and family conflict.
Structures preserve capital; governance preserves the family that
owns it. Rockefeller-vs-Vanderbilt is the canonical contrast.

## The Instruments (Tier 7–8 territory, principles apply earlier)

- **Family mission statement** — one page: what the wealth is for,
  what the family values, what it will and won't fund. Every
  governance decision references it.
- **Family constitution** — the operating rules: who can be employed
  by family entities and under what qualifications, how distributions
  are decided, how disputes are resolved, how members enter/exit.
- **Family council** — the recurring meeting where the constitution
  is executed: reports from the entities, education sessions, votes.
  Cadence is the point; a constitution that never convenes is a
  framed poster.
- **Heir education** — the real succession asset: financial literacy,
  roles in family entities, apprenticeships inside the family bank or
  foundation. Structured so heirs meet the money as stewards, not
  lottery winners (quiet trusts can delay visibility; see
  [Residency and Domestic Havens](residency-and-domestic-havens.md)).
- **Trustee architecture** — successor trustees named and *briefed*;
  directed trusts split admin/investment/distribution; at scale a
  **Private Trust Company** (PTC, sited in SD/WY) lets the family be
  its own trustee — with the hard rule that a PTC needs demonstrated
  independence: a trust cannot be managed by a company the
  beneficiaries entirely control without collapsing its protection.

## The Three Pillars Synthesis

The corpus's consolidated model for durable family wealth:

1. **A capital engine** — e.g., the family banking system (see
   [Infinite Banking and Family Banking](infinite-banking-and-family-bank.md)).
2. **A liability firewall** — the holding-company stack (see
   [Entity Structuring and Domicile](entity-structuring-and-domicile.md)).
3. **A governance/estate shield** — the trust system plus the
   constitution that tells generations how to run 1 and 2.

The pillars activate progressively: capital engine ~Tier 4–5,
holding-company layering ~Tier 5–6, the full trust/governance system
~Tier 6–8.

## What This Looks Like Below Tier 7

Governance principles scale down: a documented letter of instruction
(see
[estate-planning-practices](../estate-planning-practices/index.md)),
named-and-briefed successor trustees, an annual family money
conversation, and a succession memo (who to call, what exists, where
documents live) deliver most of the benefit without PTC machinery.

## Cautions

- **Governance without assets is theater** — councils and
  constitutions pay off at Tier 7+; earlier, a letter of instruction
  and a will do the job.
- **The 70% statistic is directional** — widely cited (Williams
  research lineage), roughly right, not precision data.
- **Independence is legally load-bearing** — a family governance
  structure that fails the independence test (self-dealing trustees,
  beneficiary-controlled PTC) can void the protections it was built
  for.
