# Founder Content System

## The One-Sentence Method

**Facts come from verified sources, voice comes from the client's verbatim
writing, style is enforced by countable checks, and every correction compounds
in a ledger.** Never write from a description of the voice.

This is the codified methodology behind the `founder-content-system` skill.
The skill is the **execution layer**; this page is the **why and what**. The
system was generalized from a live founder-ghostwriting engagement and has
been tested across multiple clients and platforms.

## Why This System Exists

Most founder content fails in one of two ways:

1. **Slop** — content that sounds like every other AI-generated post. No
   anchor, no substance, no voice. A post anchored on a real number cannot
   say nothing — slop is mostly a substance problem.
2. **Generic voice** — content that is factually correct but reads in a
   median register. Every adjective used to "describe the voice" maps to the
   same median output. Copy real sentences instead.

This system solves both by separating facts (verified), voice (verbatim),
style (countable), and corrections (compounding). The separation is the
innovation — most systems conflate them and produce content that is neither
accurate nor in-voice.

## The Five-Phase Pipeline

### Phase 0 — Intake Inventory

Before any research, inventory the raw inputs and report the gaps. Do not
silently skip a research stream because its pointer is missing. Every input
is optional; every gap is named.

The 13 inputs:

| # | Input | Why the system needs it |
|---|-------|------------------------|
| 1 | Recorded insight call (45–60 min, transcribed) | Single richest source of voice, facts, and constraints |
| 2 | Founder social handles (X, LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, blog) | Needed to scrape verbatim posts for the voice corpus |
| 3 | Reference links (accounts and posts the founder admires + WHY) | Defines structural imitation target; the WHY separates structure from surface |
| 4 | Founder's existing posts (full text) | The voice corpus is built from verbatim samples, never from a description |
| 5 | Published research / technical material | Claims must trace to primary documents, not intermediate summaries |
| 6 | Other transcripts (sales, customer, roadmap, podcasts, talks) | Each is a voice and fact source; spoken voice is pre-approved material |
| 7 | Everything the founder has published | Each is a voice and anchor source; published content is pre-approved |
| 8 | Verbatim customer quotes and real numbers | Anchored posts need real numbers; slop is mostly a substance problem |
| 9 | Constraints in writing (nameable customers, banned topics, competitor rules, who approves) | The persona constraints file is built from these |
| 10 | Reference accounts they admire and WHY | Defines structural imitation vs. what their persona cannot do |
| 11 | Brand or positioning doc | Grounds the facts layer and persona constraints |
| 12 | Research or technical papers (the actual PDFs) | Agent summaries drift from primary sources in BOTH directions |
| 13 | Internal docs (manifesto-equivalents, launch docs) | Internal docs go stale; read the LIVE version even when a file copy exists |

Deliver the inventory as a FOUND / MISSING checklist and proceed on what is
found while the gaps get filled asynchronously.

### Phase 1 — Facts Layer (before any writing)

1. **Context docs** (3–5 files): company story, product/technical glossary,
   positioning & audience, customers & track record. Mark every unverified
   item `[UNVERIFIED]` inline. When two internal sources give different
   numbers, record BOTH with attribution — never silently pick one. These are
   living documents: every new call transcript triggers a re-read and update.
2. **Primary sources folder**: download the actual papers/reports/PDFs. Claims
   in posts must trace to the primary document, not to an intermediate
   summary. Enumerate through EVERY channel (company site, Hugging Face org,
   GitHub org READMEs, arXiv author search). Verify downloads are real PDFs.
3. **Live-version check**: for any Google Doc/Drive link, read the LIVE
   version even when a file copy exists — internal docs go stale.
4. **Anchor bank**: every post starts from a sourced anchor (verbatim client
   quote, verified stat, dated public artifact). No anchor, no draft. Strike
   out used anchors. Section for embargoed claims.

### Phase 2 — Voice Layer

1. **Scrape the client's real posts verbatim** — full text, not summaries.
   Identify which era of their posting represents the voice they WANT (one
   client's older posts were a voice they had moved on from).
2. **Capture spoken voice** from call transcripts — founders explain their
   product best out loud; those phrasings are pre-approved material.
3. **Collect reference accounts they admire** and the WHY (what to imitate
   structurally vs. what their persona cannot do).
4. **Write the Voice Corpus file**: verbatim samples annotated with the
   specific move each makes, a voice-DNA summary derived from (never imposed
   on) the samples, and a post-type table mapping formats to model samples.
5. **Measure calibrations — never carry another client's numbers across.**
   Per client: punchline budget, hedge density and hedge words, emotional
   temperature, emoji/formatting habits, sentence-length range, analogy type,
   platform register differences.

### Phase 3 — Rules Layer (three files)

1. **Persona constraints**: what the client structurally can/cannot say —
   competitor naming, customer NDA status, banned company narratives,
   terminology preferences, audience priority order, review workflow. Comes
   from the calls, not from guessing.
2. **Banned patterns (slop lint)**: seed with the universal list (below),
   then grow client-specific entries via the ledger.
3. **Humanizer (stylometric pass)**: copy the Gate E structure from a
   reference implementation, refill baselines with this client's
   measurements. Includes the rewrite-pair library, seeded empty, grown from
   reviews.

### Phase 4 — Drafting Workflow (the gates)

The pipeline that must survive intact:

0. **Topic plan with adversarial critique, approved BEFORE drafting.** Attack
   your own list: peg older than a month? contradicts public positioning?
   competitor adjacency? repeats a recent post? Batch size comes from the
   client's committed cadence.
1. **Anchor gate** — no invented facts; gaps become client questions, never
   filler.
2. **Format worksheet before drafting** — the idea dictates the container.
   Batch format budget: ≥4x length range, no two posts sharing
   opener+closer type, at most two long essays, at least one sub-50-word
   post. Platform variants must differ in content and register, not just
   length.
3. **Isolated drafting** — one clean-context agent per post. Drafting a batch
   in one context converges every post to one rhythm. This is the exact
   failure isolated drafting exists to prevent.
4. **Gate A accuracy** — claim→primary source; dates are claims; opinions
   trace to stated positions; public facts are OUR job to verify.
5. **Gates B–E** — banned patterns, sameness check, voice pass, stylometric
   humanizer.
6. **Cold adversarial review** — a fresh-context agent holding ONLY the
   client's verbatim posts + ban list + rewrite pairs reviews the drafts with
   zero knowledge of their drafting. The writer cannot review itself; same
   model, same instincts, fixes regress to the same defaults. This step took
   the process from four client style-rounds to zero on the test batch.
7. **Delivery format**: post text + claims-with-sources block + open items.
   Scheduling notes: sibling posts a week apart, two benchmark posts never
   adjacent, thesis-sharing posts separated, launch-window posts placed
   relative to launch date.

### Phase 5 — Feedback Loop

- **Feedback ledger**, entry format: BEFORE (verbatim) / AFTER (verbatim) /
  TYPE / RULE (generalized) / STATUS (promoted where). Update in the SAME
  session that implements any feedback round — client edits in their own hand
  are the highest-value entries.
- Patterns appearing twice get promoted to the ban list or constraints.
- **The client's taste, and yours, is the training signal.** The system
  exists to make each correction permanent.
- **The README stays true in the same session as any structural change.** The
  map falling behind the territory is the exact failure this system exists to
  prevent.

## The Day-of Reactive Track

Evergreen batches go through review cycles; news takes decay in 48h. Reactive
process: find the live peg (browse X same day), verify the primary artifact,
draft in-voice with translations for non-expert readers, deliver same day via
Slack. Peg-age rule: nothing older than ~a month gets reaction framing; older
references get dated-assessment framing ("Back in May, X predicted...").

## The Universal Banned-Pattern List

Transferable across all clients. Client-specific entries get added per
engagement via the feedback ledger.

**Core style rules:** no dashes; no staccato chains; active voice; banned
words (shift, gap, leap, gains, solid, practical, clicked, shifted, "the
result was clear:"); no vague statements; no "it's not x, it's y" or any
antithesis/negation-mirror/then-now template; no question segues; no "The
focus is simple."-family phrases; no "No speeches. No discussion." format;
plain operational language; nothing with "actually matter".

**Learned universals:** no dramatized pre-framing (state the fact, the number
does the flinching) · no poetic metaphor (mechanical/institutional analogies
only) · no wistful/confessional closers · no symmetric antithesis machines ·
punchline stacking = strongest AI signature (budget measured per client) · no
glue sentences (every sentence carries a fact) · quarry narrative posts from
real transcript sentences, never generate connective tissue · no press-release
verbs (earmarked, unveiled, slated) · no abstract back-referents ("results
like that") · introduce every named artifact, translate mechanisms to plain
words · no positioning tropes ("sits at the intersection of") · no engineered
thesis sentences (short claim, then everyday mechanism) · abstraction subjects
must be able to do their verbs (money can't hire, checking can't be people) ·
no sweeping absolutes ("nobody is talking about") · no folksy idioms outside
the client's measured casual register · reader-biography claims must hold for
the whole audience · dates/temporal markers only from sources · no
withheld-punchline structure (setup → beat → bare-number reveal); state the
claim in sentence one and explain · category words hide the thing; concrete
instances first — but verify "concrete" against the client's register · no
"us included" style mid-sentence self-inclusion parentheticals · conventional
≠ crafted: the client's ordinary pleasantries and repetitions are allowed;
crafted language is the tell.

## Meta-Lessons (Why Guides Fail and This Works)

1. **Guides describe voice in adjectives; every adjective maps to the same
   median output.** Copy real sentences instead.
2. **Slop is mostly a substance problem:** a post anchored on a real number
   cannot say nothing.
3. **The register discovery must be re-made per client by DIFFING drafts
   against their real posts sentence-by-sentence BEFORE first delivery.** For
   one client it was "ordinary sentences, special ideas"; another founder's
   may differ, but there will be one, and finding it late cost four review
   rounds.
4. **Fine-tune upgrade path:** once 30–50 published, ledger-refined posts
   exist, a LoRA on the client's corpus becomes viable; the gates remain
   either way.

## Subagent Dependency

The system runs on subagents. The deep web crawl in Phase 1, the papers
enumeration in Phase 1.2, and above all the isolated drafting in Phase 4.3 —
one clean context per post — all depend on spawning agents in parallel. A
batch of five posts can mean a dozen or more agent runs.

**The first time the system runs for a client, ask once.** State roughly how
many agents the work implies, then get a yes. After that the approval stands
for the engagement. Never quietly substitute single-context drafting because
spawning felt presumptuous — that is the exact failure Phase 4.3 exists to
prevent. If parallel agents are refused or unavailable, say plainly what
degrades: a batch drafted in one context converges on a single rhythm, and
the cold review in Phase 4.6 is the one step that cannot be dropped without
losing the quality guarantee.

## Platform Coverage

The system produces content for seven platforms, each with a different
register and constraint set:

| Platform | Register | Cadence (active founder) |
|----------|----------|--------------------------|
| X (Twitter) | Punchy, conversational, opinion-forward | 3–7 posts/workweek |
| LinkedIn | Longer-form, reflective, professional-but-personal | 2–4 posts/workweek |
| Instagram | Visual-first; caption is context | 3–5 posts/week |
| TikTok | Casual, direct-to-camera, trend-aware | 3–7 posts/week |
| YouTube | Educational or narrative; longer attention span | 1–2 long-form/week or 3–5 shorts/week |
| Facebook | Community-oriented; longer narrative in groups | 1–3 posts/week |
| Blog | Longest form; full argument with evidence | 1–2 posts/month |

**Platform variants of one story must differ in content and register, not
just length.** A LinkedIn post shortened to fit X is not an X post. The voice
corpus captures the founder's per-platform register; calibrations are
measured per-platform, not carried across.
