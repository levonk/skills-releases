# Genetic and Biomarker Testing

## Why It Matters Early

Molecular profiling (biomarker testing of the tumor) and germline
genetic testing determine which targeted therapies, immunotherapies,
and clinical trials apply to your case. Without the results, the
standard-of-care tree can't be fully traversed — see
[Standard of Care Navigation](standard-of-care-navigation.md).

## The Failure Mode: Promised but Not Ordered

Some doctors are slow or inconsistent about ordering comprehensive
testing — in the seed case, a surgeon who said he'd get it done
eventually referred out to an oncologist, and the first test was run
on the wrong specimen (the surgery tumor rather than the biopsy
tumor), causing a **multi-month delay** in usable genetic
results.[^maxwell-cancer-2026]

**Countermeasure:** put the order on the follow-up list from
[Scheduling Persistence](scheduling-persistence.md). Ask explicitly:

- "Has comprehensive biomarker/molecular testing been ordered? On
  which specimen?"
- "Should I get genetic counseling and germline testing — for me and
  potentially for my family?" (an ACS standard
  question[^acs-questions])
- "When will results be back, and who reviews them with me?"

## Practical Points

- **Ask which panel.** "Comprehensive genomic profiling" panels cover
  hundreds of markers; a single-marker test may miss the actionable
  one. Ask what the panel covers and why.
- **Specimen matters.** Confirm which tissue the lab will use —
  biopsy vs. surgical specimen can yield different windows into the
  tumor, and using the wrong one can burn months.
- **Results change options.** A positive marker can open a targeted
  drug or a trial; a negative result prunes the tree. Either way it is
  decision-relevant information worth pushing for.
- **Family implication.** Germline findings may indicate hereditary
  risk — relevant to relatives, and a reason genetic counseling exists
  as a distinct service.

## Common Mistakes

- **Assuming "the doctor said they'd order it" means it was ordered.**
  Verify the order exists; verify the lab received the specimen;
  verify the result came back.
- **Treating testing as optional.** For most solid tumors, biomarker
  status is now part of staging-level decision-making.
- **Not asking about trial gating.** Many trials require specific
  markers — results are often a prerequisite for
  [Clinical Trials](clinical-trials.md) eligibility screening.
