# Standard of Care Navigation

## The Core Practice

Every major cancer type has a published "standard of care" — the
evidence-based decision tree oncologists actually follow. In the US,
the canonical source is the **NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines**:
flow charts that walk from diagnosis characteristics to recommended
treatment for each stage.[^nccn-guidelines]

Read the flow charts yourself. Knowing the standard of care before the
oncology visit converts you from passenger to informed participant —
you can ask "we're at node X; why is the plan Y instead of Z?"

## How to Access

- **NCCN professional guidelines** — free account required;
  non-professionals can register. The PDF versions are the easiest to
  work through; an interactive navigator also
  exists.[^maxwell-cancer-2026]
- **NCCN Guidelines for Patients** — plain-language versions of the
  same decision trees, including "questions to ask" lists (e.g., "Are
  you suggesting options other than what NCCN recommends? If yes,
  why?").[^nccn-patient]

## Mapping Your Case to the Tree

To place yourself on the flow chart you need: cancer type/subtype,
stage, grade, biomarker status (see
[Genetic and Biomarker Testing](genetic-and-biomarker-testing.md)),
and prior treatments. This is exactly the kind of structured matching
AI does well — feed the actual pathology report and the relevant NCCN
page to the model and ask it to trace the path, but verify against the
guideline document itself (see
[AI Assistance Caveats](ai-assistance-caveats.md)).

## What to Do With Divergence

If the care team's plan differs from the guideline:

1. **Ask why directly.** There are often legitimate reasons —
  comorbidities, newer trial data, institutional expertise. The
  question is not an accusation; NCCN itself lists it as a standard
  patient question.
2. **Note it for the second opinion.** Divergence from standard of
  care is precisely what a
  [second opinion](second-opinions-and-cancer-centers.md) should
  adjudicate.
3. **Check whether a trial explains it.** "Better than standard" is
  what trials are for — see [Clinical Trials](clinical-trials.md).

## Common Mistakes

- **Assuming the care team is following the tree.** Usually they are;
  occasionally they aren't, and you won't know unless you look.
- **Reading the professional version only.** The patient versions are
  faster to absorb and contain question lists engineered for
  non-clinicians.
- **Treating the guideline as a mandate.** It is a baseline for the
  conversation — deviations should be *explained*, not forbidden.
