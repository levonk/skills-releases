# Cancer Patient Practices — Overview

This bundle synthesizes guidance for the **patient side of a cancer
diagnosis** — the things a newly diagnosed patient (or their caregiver)
must actively drive, because the healthcare system will not drive them
for you.

The seed source is a first-person account of a 2026 oral squamous cell
carcinoma diagnosis[^maxwell-cancer-2026], augmented with institutional
patient guidance from the NCI, ACS, NCCN, and NIA. The practices are
general — they apply to most solid-tumor and blood-cancer diagnoses in
the US healthcare system. Nothing here is medical advice; it is
*navigation* advice — how to get the right medical advice faster.

## The Core Thesis

**No one cares more about your case than you.** The system's default
state is delay: appointments scheduled weeks out, tests not ordered,
questions unanswered. Every concept in this bundle exists to close the
gap between what the system does by default and what a well-informed,
persistent patient can make it do.

The second thesis: **speed and quality concentrate at major cancer
centers.** Pathology re-reads in 20 minutes instead of never, surgeons
who have done your procedure hundreds of times, tumor boards that
review your case the same week. The cost is a drive or a flight; the
benefit is measured in months and outcomes.

## The Navigation Map

```
Diagnosis → Confirm details → Assemble records → Second opinion at a
   major cancer center → Molecular/genetic testing → Read the NCCN
   standard of care → Decide treatment → Execute → Follow-up
                                   ↘ (if standard of care runs out)
                                     Clinical trials → Palliative care
                                     → Hospice → Estate/end-of-life
```

## What This Bundle Is Not

- **Not medical advice.** It tells you how to find, evaluate, and
  pressure-test medical advice — not what treatment to choose.
- **Not prognosis.** It does not tell you how long anyone has.
- **Not a substitute for the care team.** It exists to make the care
  team work better for the patient.
